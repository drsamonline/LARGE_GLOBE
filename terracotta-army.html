The Terracotta Army

Thousands of life-size soldiers buried with the First Emperor, individually modelled and painted, found by farmers digging a well.

The find

In 1974 farmers digging a well near Xi'an found fragments of terracotta figures. Excavation revealed pits containing thousands of life-size soldiers, horses and chariots, buried around 210 BC for the tomb of Qin Shi Huang.

Maps, diagrams and images

The figures

The figures are individually modelled, with different faces, hair and equipment by rank, and were originally painted. They were armed with real weapons, many of which were looted. The main tomb itself has not been excavated.

The archive files it as documented because the excavation and the analysis are published.

What is established

Documented: the pits and the figures.

Documented: the individual modelling.

Documented: the original paint.

Documented: the looting of weapons.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most famous excavation in the world, and the detail is better than the legend.
