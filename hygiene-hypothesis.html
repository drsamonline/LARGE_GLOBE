The Hygiene Hypothesis

The proposal that too-clean childhoods leave the immune system under-trained. The epidemiology is suggestive and the mechanism is argued.

The proposal

David Strachan proposed in 1989 that childhood infections and a less hygienic environment protect against allergic disease, based on the observation that allergies were less common in larger families. The 'old friends' variant emphasises exposure to harmless microbes and parasites rather than infections.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Farm exposure, pet ownership and helminth infection are associated with lower allergy rates in many studies, and the microbiome is implicated. The associations are inconsistent across populations, and trials of probiotics and helminth therapy have had mixed results.

The archive files it as live debate.

Where it stands

Documented: the epidemiological associations.

Supported: the farm and pet effects.

Mixed: the trials.

Contested: the mechanism.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a claim that changed parenting advice before it was settled.
