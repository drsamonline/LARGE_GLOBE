The Wandjina

Rock art from the Kimberley shows figures with large white faces and haloes. They were read as astronauts in helmets. They are ancestral beings, painted by the people who still paint them.

The art

Wandjina figures appear in rock art and bark paintings across the Kimberley region of Western Australia. They are depicted with large white faces, dark eyes and a halo or headdress, and they are ancestral beings associated with rain and with the creation of the landscape. The tradition is living: the paintings are repainted in ceremony by the Ngarinyin, Wunambal and Worora peoples.

Maps, diagrams and images

The claim

In the ancient-astronaut literature the figures were read as astronauts in helmets or as visitors in spacesuits. The reading requires ignoring the tradition that explains them and the artists who maintain it.

The 'helmet' is a headdress or halo; the 'goggles' are the eyes of a being associated with cloud and rain; and the white pigment is kaolin, used for its ritual significance. The interpretation is not uncertain — it is documented by the people whose heritage it is.

What is established

Documented: the art, its antiquity, and its continuing tradition.

Documented: the meaning, from the communities who paint it.

Not established: any connection to astronauts or spacesuits.

The error: reading an unfamiliar iconography through a modern visual vocabulary.

The harm: the claim strips the art from the people who made it and gives it to outsiders.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the clearest case in this collection where the debunking is not only factual but ethical: the explanation was available from the artists all along.
