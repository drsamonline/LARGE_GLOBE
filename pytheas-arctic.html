Pytheas in the Arctic

A Greek navigator sailed to Britain, described sea ice and a frozen sea, and was disbelieved for centuries.

The voyage

Pytheas of Massalia, around 325 BC, sailed to Britain and beyond, describing the tin trade, the length of the days in the north, and a 'congealed sea' — sea ice. His account survives only in quotations by later writers, some of whom doubted him.

Maps, diagrams and images

The reception

Strabo called him a liar, and the description of sea ice was one reason. Modern assessment treats the voyage as substantially credible, with the ice description matching Arctic conditions and the latitude reports consistent with northern waters.

The archive files it as documented because the quotations and the plausibility of the account are on the record.

What is established

Documented: the voyage, through quotations.

Documented: the ice description.

Documented: the ancient disbelief.

Supported: the account's plausibility.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where the traveller was right and the scholars were wrong.
