Yang–Mills and the Mass Gap

Quantum field theory works, and nobody can prove that its simplest version is mathematically consistent — or that its particles have mass.

The problem

Yang–Mills theory is the framework of the strong and electroweak interactions. The Millennium Prize problem asks for a rigorous mathematical construction of a Yang–Mills theory in four dimensions that satisfies the axioms of quantum field theory and has a mass gap — a lowest energy above zero, which is why the particles have mass.

Maps, diagrams and images

Why it is hard

Quantum field theories are notoriously difficult to define rigorously; even the simpler cases required decades of work. The mass gap is the physical content: in pure Yang–Mills theory the excitations are massive despite the theory having no mass parameter, which is the phenomenon of dimensional transmutation.

Lattice calculations confirm the mass gap numerically; a proof is another matter.

Where it stands

Used: as the basis of particle physics.

Confirmed numerically: on the lattice.

Open: a rigorous construction with a mass gap.

Prize: Millennium Prize.

The label: open problem.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the clearest case of a theory that predicts correctly and has no mathematical foundation, which is a useful thing to know about physics.
