Stone Softening in the Andes

A claim that Inca masons softened stone with plant acids to fit it perfectly. The fit is real; the method is not established.

The claim

The claim holds that Inca builders softened andesite and granite with plant acids or by heating, so that blocks could be moulded to fit, which would explain the tight joints and irregular shapes of Inca walls.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The joints are documented and extraordinary, and the fitting was achieved by patient shaping, trial and error — a process visible in unfinished blocks. The softening hypothesis has been tested experimentally and does not produce the observed surfaces; the acids available would not dissolve andesite at the rates required.

The archive files it as fringe: a real observation with an untested mechanism.

Where it stands

Documented: the masonry and its precision.

Documented: unfinished blocks showing shaping.

Tested: softening, without success.

Not established: any chemical method.

The honest label: fringe.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best-known 'lost technique' claim, and the unfinished stones answer it.
