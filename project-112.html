Project 112 and SHAD

The US military tested biological and chemical agents on ships and in the open, sometimes without informing the personnel involved. The programme was disclosed decades later.

The programme

Project 112 was a US Department of Defense programme that ran from 1962 to 1973, testing biological and chemical agents in the open — including at sea, on ships, and in several land locations. A sub-programme, SHAD (Shipboard Hazard and Defense), tested agents on naval vessels with crews aboard, in some cases without telling them what was being released.

The disclosure

The programme was declassified in stages, and a 2002 Pentagon review found that thousands of service members may have been exposed. The department set up a register and contacted veterans; several tests had used live biological agents and simulants.

The scale and the secrecy are documented; the health consequences are harder to establish, which is the ongoing argument.

What is established

Documented: the programme, its tests and its locations.

Documented: the 2002 review and the veteran register.

Contested: the health effects on participants.

Documented: the absence of consent in some tests.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the conspiracy claim — the military tested agents on its own personnel — was substantially correct.
