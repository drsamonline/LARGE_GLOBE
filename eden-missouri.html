Eden in Missouri

A claim that the Garden of Eden was in Missouri, with a stone altar to prove it. The stone is real; the garden is not.

The claim

A nineteenth-century movement taught that the Garden of Eden was in Daviess County, Missouri, and pointed to a stone altar as evidence. The site is maintained and visited.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

The altar is a real object of uncertain date, and the identification rests on the movement's scripture rather than on archaeology. No evidence connects the location to the Genesis narrative, which is not a geography.

Where it stands

Documented: the site and the stone.

Documented: the movement's teaching.

Not established: any link to Genesis.

Faith claim: not archaeology.

The honest label: fiction.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the American version of Eden geography, and it has an altar.
