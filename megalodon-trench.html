Megalodon in the Trench

A giant shark known from teeth, extinct for millions of years, said to survive in the deep ocean. The size of the animal is the argument against it.

The animal

Otodus megalodon was a shark that lived from roughly 23 to 3.6 million years ago, known from teeth and some vertebrae. Estimates of its length range from 15 to 20 metres. It was a coastal predator, and its teeth are common in fossil deposits.

Maps, diagrams and images

The claim

The survival claim holds that megalodon persists in the deep ocean, where it has escaped detection. The objections are ecological and practical: a predator of that size needs a large population and a large prey base, it would leave teeth and carcasses, and its teeth are distinctive enough that a single recent specimen would settle the question.

The fossil record shows a decline and disappearance, and the 'deep ocean' is increasingly well surveyed.

What is established

Documented: the species, its size and its extinction.

Documented: its coastal habitat.

Not found: any recent tooth or specimen.

Not plausible: a viable population of that size undetected.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best-known 'extinct animal still alive' claim, and because a single tooth would settle it.
