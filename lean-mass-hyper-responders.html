Lean Mass Hyper-Responders

A subgroup on low-carbohydrate diets shows large rises in LDL cholesterol while remaining lean and metabolically healthy. Whether that is harmless is the question.

The phenotype

The lean mass hyper-responder phenotype, described from the late 2010s, is defined by three features on a low-carbohydrate or ketogenic diet: high LDL cholesterol, high HDL cholesterol and low triglycerides, in a person who is lean. It is a recognised pattern in lipidology.

Maps, diagrams and images

The question

The argument is whether the high LDL carries the usual cardiovascular risk in this group. The conventional view is that LDL is causal in atherosclerosis regardless of the pattern; the alternative view is that the metabolic context matters and that this phenotype is not harmful.

The evidence is observational and small, and a prospective study is under way. Until it reports, the question is open, and the conventional view is the safer default.

Where it stands

Documented: the lipid pattern.

Contested: the long-term risk.

Conventional view: LDL remains causal.

Alternative view: context matters.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a new and specific claim in a field where the general principle is well established, and because the answer is not yet in.
