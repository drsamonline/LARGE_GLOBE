Flexible Glass

The story of a glass that could be bent, and an inventor executed to protect the gold trade. It is a legend with no evidence.

The story

A legend, recorded in Roman writers, tells of an inventor who brought a glass vessel that could be bent and hammered without breaking to Tiberius; the emperor had him executed to prevent the value of gold from falling.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

The story appears in late compilations and is repeated without corroboration. No such glass exists in the archaeological record, and the chemistry required — a ductile glass — is not known from antiquity. The tale is a moral anecdote about innovation and power.

Where it stands

Documented: the legend.

Not found: any such glass.

Not known: the chemistry.

Literary: the function.

The honest label: fiction.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the ancient world's most famous lost invention, and it has no artefact.
