The Tic Tac

A Navy pilot filmed an object off San Diego in 2004 that looked like a tic tac and appeared to move impossibly. It is the best-documented modern UAP case, and it is still unexplained.

The encounter

In November 2004, during exercises off San Diego, the USS Nimitz carrier group tracked objects descending from about 24,000 metres to sea level. A flight of F/A-18s intercepted one; the gun camera recorded a white, tic-tac-shaped object that appeared to accelerate away without visible exhaust.

The analyses

The video is genuine and was released officially. Analyses have proposed several mundane explanations: a parallax effect from the camera's tracking, a distant aircraft or balloon, sensor artefacts, or a classified drone. None has been demonstrated conclusively, and the pilots' accounts of the object's behaviour have not been explained.

The case is significant because of the quality of the data — radar, infrared video, and multiple trained observers — not because it establishes anything.

Where it stands

Documented: the video, the radar tracks and the witness accounts.

Contested: the mundane explanations.

Not established: any exotic craft.

Not established: the object's performance as described.

The honest label: live debate — the best case in the collection for 'unidentified' meaning unidentified.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the one page where the archive's answer is genuinely 'we do not know', and because that is a category worth having.
