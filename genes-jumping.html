Genes Jumping Between Animals

Genes that move between species, not just from parent to child. Horizontal transfer is documented; its importance is argued.

The phenomenon

Horizontal gene transfer — the movement of genetic material between organisms other than by descent — is common in bacteria and documented in animals, including transposons acquired from other species and genes from parasites or prey.

Maps, diagrams and images

The debate

Examples are documented, including transferred transposons in insects and mammals. How much of animal evolution depends on such transfers is argued: some researchers see it as a significant source of novelty, others as rare and mostly neutral.

Where it stands

Documented: horizontal transfer in animals.

Documented: specific examples.

Contested: its evolutionary importance.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it complicates the tree of life that biology teaching still draws as a tree.
