Zero-Click Search Extinction

More searches now end without a click, because the answer appears on the results page or in an AI summary. Whether that is the death of the open web or an ordinary shift is argued.

The claim

The argument is that search engines and AI assistants increasingly answer questions directly — featured snippets, knowledge panels, and now generated summaries — so users do not click through to publishers. On this reading, the referral traffic that funds independent publishing is being withdrawn.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Studies of search behaviour have found that a large share of queries end without a click, and publishers report declining referral traffic. The measurement is contested because the platforms do not publish the data, and because 'zero-click' includes queries that never had a clickable answer.

The structural point is not in dispute: if the answer is on the results page, the publisher does not get the visit. Whether that ends the open web or simply changes its business model is the argument.

Where it stands

Documented: the shift to direct answers.

Documented: declining referral traffic, in publisher reports.

Contested: the scale, and the definition of a zero-click search.

Contested: the long-term effect on publishing.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a claim about the internet's economics made almost entirely from data held by the companies being described.
