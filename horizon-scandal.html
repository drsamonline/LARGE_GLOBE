The Horizon Scandal

A faulty accounting system made subpostmasters look like thieves. Hundreds were prosecuted, some went to prison, and the Post Office fought the victims for twenty years.

The system

The Post Office's Horizon accounting system, introduced in 1999, recorded shortfalls that subpostmasters could not explain. The Post Office prosecuted hundreds of them for theft and false accounting, based on the system's output, and many were convicted, fined, or imprisoned.

Maps, diagrams and images

The exposure

Subpostmasters organised, and a 2019 High Court case found that the system contained bugs and errors and that the Post Office's own investigators knew of them. A public inquiry began in 2022, and legislation was introduced to quash convictions automatically.

By 2024 more than a hundred convictions had been overturned, and the inquiry heard evidence that the Post Office had withheld information and treated its own operators as adversaries for years.

What is established

Documented: the system's faults, the prosecutions and the convictions.

Documented: the 2019 judgment and the public inquiry.

Documented: the overturned convictions.

Contested: individual culpability at senior levels.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where the institution was the wrongdoer and the evidence was a computer log — and because it took twenty years.
