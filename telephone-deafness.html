The Telephone and Deafness

Early telephones were said to damage hearing and cause 'telephone deafness'. The claim did not survive measurement.

The claim

In the early decades of telephony, medical writers warned that the receiver's loud clicks and constant use would damage hearing, and the condition 'telephone deafness' was described in journals.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Audiometric studies found no characteristic hearing loss in telephone operators, and the concern faded as the technology became ordinary. The episode is documented as an early example of a technology-health panic.

What is established

Documented: the concern.

Documented: the studies.

Not found: a characteristic deafness.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the first entry in a long series that continues to the present day.
