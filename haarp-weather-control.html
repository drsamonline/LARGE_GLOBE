HAARP Weather Control

A research facility in Alaska heats a patch of the upper atmosphere with radio waves. It has been blamed for earthquakes, hurricanes and mind control, and its power output is smaller than a large city's.

The facility

HAARP — the High-frequency Active Auroral Research Program — is an ionospheric research facility at Gakona, Alaska, with a phased array of 180 antennas and a transmitter of about 3.6 megawatts. It heats a patch of the ionosphere to study its physics, including radio propagation and auroral processes.

Maps, diagrams and images

The claim

The conspiracy literature attributes earthquakes, hurricanes, droughts and mind control to HAARP. The objection is scale: a weather system contains energies many orders of magnitude larger than HAARP's output, and the ionosphere is 100 km up and does not couple to the lower atmosphere in the way the claims require.

The facility's funding was ended in 2013 and it was transferred to the University of Alaska Fairbanks in 2015, which is a strange fate for a supposed global weapon.

What is established

Documented: the facility, its power and its research programme.

Documented: its transfer to a university after funding ended.

Not established: any weather or seismic effect.

Not plausible: the energies required, by orders of magnitude.

The honest label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where a real facility with a strange name did most of the work, and the numbers finish it.
