The Dogon and Sirius B

The Dogon of Mali are said to have known that Sirius has a tiny, dense companion with a fifty-year orbit, centuries before telescopes could see it. The fieldwork behind the claim is the problem.

The claim

The Dogon are said to know that Sirius has a companion star, Sirius B — invisible to the naked eye — with an orbital period of about fifty years, and that it is extremely dense. The claim comes from the fieldwork of Marcel Griaule and Germaine Dieterlen in the 1930s–1940s, published in the 1950s.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

Sirius B's orbit and density were established by astronomy in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, so the information was available in print before Griaule's work. The Dogon's own traditions, as recorded by later anthropologists, are more ambiguous than the claim requires, and Griaule's methods — long conversations with a small number of informants, translated and interpreted — have been criticised as leading.

The alternative explanations are contamination from European sources, or a reading imposed by the anthropologist. The extraterrestrial-teacher version requires a third explanation with no evidence.

What is established

Documented: the Dogon's astronomical traditions, as recorded.

Documented: the timing — Sirius B's properties were known in print first.

Contested: how precise the Dogon knowledge actually was.

Not established: any pre-telescopic knowledge, or any teacher.

The honest label: debunked, on the documentary record.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best-constructed version of the ancient-knowledge claim, and it fails on a date rather than on a mystery.
