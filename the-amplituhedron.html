The Amplituhedron

A geometric object whose volume encodes particle-scattering probabilities. It works, and it is not yet physics in the usual sense.

The object

In 2013 Nima Arkani-Hamed and Jaroslav Trnka described a geometric object whose volume gives the probabilities of particle scattering in a certain theory. The amplituhedron reproduces results that previously required summing enormous numbers of Feynman diagrams.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The construction works in a simplified theory, not in the real world, and it assumes supersymmetry. It is not yet known whether the approach extends to realistic physics, and the geometric picture's physical meaning is unclear — locality and unitarity emerge from it rather than being built in.

The archive files it as live debate: a real mathematical advance whose physical status is open.

Where it stands

Documented: the construction and its results.

Documented: the simplification it relies on.

Open: extension to realistic theories.

Unclear: the physical interpretation.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the mathematics is beautiful and the physics is not yet there.
