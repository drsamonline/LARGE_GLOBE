5G and COVID-19

A claim that mobile networks caused or spread the disease. It led to attacks on masts and to real harm.

The claim

The claim held that 5G networks caused or transmitted COVID-19, or that symptoms were radiation effects. It spread in early 2020 and was linked to arson attacks on mobile masts in several countries.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

Viruses and radio waves are different things; the disease spread in regions without 5G and did not spread in patterns matching network rollout; and the claimed mechanisms have no basis in physics or biology. Studies found no association.

The archive files it as debunked, and notes the real-world harm.

What is established

Documented: the claim and its spread.

Documented: the attacks on infrastructure.

Documented: the absence of any association.

Not established: any mechanism.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a false claim that damaged property and delayed public health work.
