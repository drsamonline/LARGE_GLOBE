Atlantis as Thera

The Minoan eruption of Santorini destroyed a civilisation, and the parallel with Plato's story is striking. The date is the problem: it is 9,000 years out.

The claim

In 1909 the archaeologist K. T. Frost suggested that the Minoan civilisation was the basis for Plato's Atlantis, and that its destruction by the Thera eruption was the catastrophe behind the story. The eruption, around 1600 BC, was one of the largest in the Holocene; it buried the town of Akrotiri and caused damage and famine on Crete.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The date is the difficulty: Plato places Atlantis's destruction 9,000 years before Solon, roughly 9,600 BC, and the Thera eruption is about 3,600 years ago. The mismatch is a factor of two and a half, and the proposed explanations — a mistranslation of Egyptian units, or a copying error — are possible but unsupported.

The cultural details also differ: Plato describes a naval empire beyond the Pillars of Hercules, and the Minoans were in the Aegean.

Where it stands

Documented: the eruption, its scale and its effects.

Documented: the Akrotiri destruction.

Contested: whether it inspired the Atlantis story.

Not supported: the date match.

The honest label: live debate, with the chronology against it.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most respectable Atlantis hypothesis, and it fails on a number rather than on a mystery.
