Grabby Aliens

A model in which civilisations expand at a fixed speed and announce themselves, and the fact that we see none constrains when they appear. It explains the Fermi paradox by predicting we are early.

The model

Robin Hanson, Daniel Martin and Jonathan McCarter proposed 'grabby aliens' in 2021. The assumptions: civilisations appear at some rate, expand at a fraction of light speed, and visibly transform the volumes they occupy — so a volume that has been settled looks different. Since we see no such volume, we must be early, and the model can be fitted to the observed absence.

Maps, diagrams and images

What it predicts

The model makes a quantitative prediction: given the observed sky, the most likely time until a grabby civilisation arrives in our volume, and the fraction of the universe already taken. The numbers that fit best put the arrival in the far future, which is consistent with what we see.

The criticism is that the model has enough free parameters to fit almost any observation, and that its predictions are about a single event — arrival — that we cannot test.

Where it stands

Documented: the model and its fit to the observations.

Contested: whether it is falsifiable.

Not tested: the arrival prediction.

Useful: as a framework for the Fermi paradox.

The honest label: fringe — a well-posed model with no test available.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most quantitative of the Fermi paradox answers, and because being quantitative is not the same as being testable.
