Sony's Rootkit

Music CDs installed hidden software that compromised the computers of the people who bought them.

The software

In 2005 Sony BMG shipped music CDs with copy-protection software that installed itself deeply in the Windows operating system, hid its files and could be exploited by malware. The software also reported listening habits.

Maps, diagrams and images

The aftermath

The security researcher who found it was threatened with legal action, then the scale of the problem became public. Sony recalled the discs, settled class actions and paid a settlement to the US states. The episode is documented in security literature and court records.
