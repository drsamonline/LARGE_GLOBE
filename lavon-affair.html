The Lavon Affair

Israeli military intelligence recruited Egyptian Jews to bomb Western targets in Egypt, in an attempt to damage relations with the West. The operation was exposed, and the political fallout lasted a decade.

The operation

In 1954, Israeli military intelligence recruited a network of Egyptian Jews to carry out bombings against American and British targets in Egypt — cinemas, post offices, a library — in an attempt to damage Egypt's relations with the West and to prevent a British withdrawal. The bombs were timed to cause damage without casualties; one device injured people.

Maps, diagrams and images

The exposure

The network was rolled up by Egyptian police, several members were tried and convicted, and two were executed. The operation became a political scandal in Israel — the 'Lavon affair', named after the defence minister — with a long dispute over who had authorised it, a resignation, and a political crisis that lasted until 1963.

What is established

Documented: the operation, the network and the trials.

Documented: the political fallout in Israel.

Contested: the exact chain of authorisation.

Documented: the executions of two members.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real false-flag operation — rare, documented, and smaller and messier than the ones imagined in the literature.
