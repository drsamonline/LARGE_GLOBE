Troy Was Real

The city in the Iliad was treated as fiction until a German businessman dug up a mound in Turkey. The site is real; the war is another question.

The site

Hisarlik in northwestern Turkey is a mound containing the remains of nine cities, occupied from the early Bronze Age to the Roman period. Excavations by Heinrich Schliemann from 1871, and later by Wilhelm Dörpfeld, Carl Blegen and Manfred Korfmann, established the sequence.

Maps, diagrams and images

The questions

Troy VI and VIIa — the latter destroyed around 1180 BC — are the candidates for the city of the Iliad. The archaeological evidence for a war includes destruction layers, but not a wooden horse, and the scale of any conflict is unknown. The Hittite texts mention a place called Wilusa, widely identified with Ilios.

The consensus is that a real city existed at a real place and was destroyed, and that the epic is a poetic composition built on distant memory.

What is established

Documented: the mound, its nine cities and their dates.

Documented: the destruction of Troy VIIa.

Documented: the Hittite references to Wilusa.

Contested: the scale and character of the war.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case that founded the discipline's public reputation: a poem led to a mound, and the mound had cities in it.
