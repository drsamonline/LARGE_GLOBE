The Trillion-Dollar Coin

A legal loophole proposed in 2011 to bypass a debt-ceiling standoff. The proposal is documented; it was never used.

The proposal

During debt-ceiling standoffs, commentators proposed that the Treasury mint a platinum coin of very high face value and deposit it at the Federal Reserve, creating room to spend without new borrowing. The idea rests on a statute allowing platinum commemorative coins with discretionary face value.

Maps, diagrams and images

The status

The proposal is documented in legal and economic commentary; the Treasury and the Fed indicated they would not use it, and it has never been attempted. Its legality is argued rather than settled.

What is established

Documented: the statute and the proposal.

Documented: the refusal to use it.

Argued: its legality.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real loophole that a serious argument was had about, and it never happened.
