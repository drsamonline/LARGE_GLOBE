The Flatwoods Monster

A group in West Virginia reported a tall figure with a red face and a hood after seeing a light in the sky. The most economical explanation is a meteor and an owl.

The event

In September 1952, children in Flatwoods, West Virginia, saw a bright object cross the sky and land. A group including adults went to investigate and reported a tall figure with a red face, a spade-shaped head and a hissing sound. One witness described a pungent smell, and several were ill afterwards.

Maps, diagrams and images

The explanations

The leading explanation combines two ordinary things: a meteor that was seen across several states that night, and a barn owl — which has a pale, heart-shaped face, hisses, and can appear large in silhouette. The illness has been attributed to anxiety and to the smell of a nearby mine or skunk.

The case is well documented as a local event, and the creature is not.

Where it stands

Documented: the sighting, the witnesses and the meteor.

Plausible: an owl and a meteor.

Contested: the details of the figure.

Not established: any creature.

The honest label: live debate, largely explained.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where two ordinary explanations fit the evidence better than one extraordinary one.
