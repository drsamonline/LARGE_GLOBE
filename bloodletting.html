Bloodletting

The standard treatment for almost everything, practised for two thousand years, and harmful.

The practice

Bloodletting was used from antiquity into the nineteenth century for fevers, inflammation and mental illness, by venesection, cupping and leeches. It followed from the humoral theory and was applied to almost every condition.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

The practice is documented, and its decline followed the realisation that it weakened patients without benefit. A famous 2006 analysis of Washington's death suggested that the bleedings contributed to it. Some limited uses in modern medicine — leeches in replantation surgery — are unrelated to the theory.

What is established

Documented: the practice.

Documented: its harms.

Explained: by the humoral theory.

Survives: only in narrow surgical uses.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it shows how long a harmful practice can survive without a trial.
