Quantitative Easing

Central banks created money to buy assets. The policy is documented; its effects are argued.

The policy

From 2008, central banks bought government and corporate bonds with newly created reserves, expanding their balance sheets by trillions. The operations are documented in central-bank accounts.

Maps, diagrams and images

The effects

Studies find that QE lowered borrowing costs and supported asset prices; effects on growth and inflation are smaller and disputed, and distributional effects are documented. Claims that it caused hyperinflation are not supported by the record.

What is established

Documented: the operations.

Documented: lower borrowing costs.

Contested: growth and inflation effects.

Not supported: hyperinflation claims.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a policy that happened, with public accounts, and it is described in ways the accounts do not support.
