The Michigan Relics

Thousands of inscribed objects 'found' in Michigan, said to record ancient migrations. They were made and planted.

The corpus

From the 1890s, thousands of objects — clay tablets, copper ornaments and inscribed slates — were reported from mounds in Michigan, bearing inscriptions in scripts that were said to record ancient peoples in America.

The problems

The objects were traced to a network of people who manufactured and planted them, including a former state archaeologist's associate; the scripts are inconsistent and the contexts were fabricated. Modern examination has confirmed the objects are modern.

The archive files it as debunked because the forgery is documented.

What is established

Documented: the corpus and its scale.

Documented: the manufacture and planting.

Documented: the inconsistent scripts.

Not established: any ancient origin.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the largest archaeological forgery in North America, and it was believed for decades.
