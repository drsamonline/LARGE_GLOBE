The Mongolian Death Worm

A worm said to live in the Gobi, capable of spitting venom or electricity. The reports come from travellers, and no specimen has ever been produced.

The reports

The creature entered Western literature through Roy Chapman Andrews's 1926 book on Central Asia, which reported the local belief in a worm that could kill at a distance. Later accounts described a red, metre-long worm that spits a corrosive fluid or discharges electricity, and that emerges after rain.

Maps, diagrams and images

The searches

Expeditions in 2005, 2009 and later searched the Gobi and interviewed herders. They found no specimen and no verified account. The Gobi's fauna is not well surveyed, but a metre-long animal with a distinctive defence would be notable.

The 'electric' element has no known biological precedent at that scale, and the venom claim would require a delivery system that no one has described.

Where it stands

Documented: the reports, and the expeditions.

Not found: any specimen, photograph or track.

Not established: the venom or electrical claims.

Not plausible: electrical discharge in a worm of that size.

The honest label: fringe.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a cryptid whose defining features are physical claims that could be tested, and have not been.
