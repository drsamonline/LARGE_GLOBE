Ötzi's Murder

A man who died in the Alps 5,300 years ago was found in 1991 with an arrowhead in his shoulder. His last days have been reconstructed in detail.

The find

In 1991 hikers in the Ötztal Alps found a body melting out of the ice. It proved to be a man who died around 3300 BC, preserved with his clothing, equipment — a copper axe, a bow, arrows — and his tattoos. He is the best-studied prehistoric body in Europe.

Maps, diagrams and images

The death

Imaging in 2005 found an arrowhead lodged in his left shoulder, with damage to a blood vessel; the wound would have been fatal. He also had a head injury, cuts to his hands, and his stomach contents showed a last meal eaten shortly before death. DNA from his equipment showed blood from other people on his knife and arrowheads.

The evidence supports a violent death, possibly after a fight or an ambush; the details are inference.

What is established

Documented: the body, the equipment and the tattoos.

Documented: the arrowhead and the fatal wound.

Documented: the last meal and the blood on his gear.

Contested: the circumstances of the fight.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the closest thing prehistory offers to a crime scene, and because the forensics are documented in the literature.
