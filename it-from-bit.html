It from Bit

Wheeler's proposal that physical reality derives from information. It is a slogan with a programme behind it.

The proposal

John Wheeler proposed that every physical quantity derives from binary answers to yes-or-no questions — 'it from bit' — and later that physics is participatory, with observers involved in constituting reality. The idea influenced quantum information and holography.

Maps, diagrams and images

The status

The slogan motivated real work in quantum information and the holographic principle, and it remains a programme rather than a theory: there is no formalism that derives physics from information in a testable way.

What the phrase claims, and what it does not

Wheeler's formulation is that every physical quantity derives its ultimate significance from yes-or-no answers to yes-or-no questions — that the universe is participatory, and information is more fundamental than matter. It is a proposal about ontology, not a theory with equations.

Its influence has been large and its content contested. The phrase is quoted in support of claims it does not make — that reality is a simulation, that consciousness creates the world — and Wheeler's own version is narrower and stranger than those. The archive keeps the page because a memorable sentence is easily detached from the argument it belonged to.
