Phrenology

The claim that personality could be read from the shape of the skull. It was wrong, and it seeded real neuroscience.

The claim

Franz Joseph Gall and his followers held that the brain was composed of organs corresponding to faculties, and that their development showed in the skull's shape. Practitioners read character from bumps and were widely consulted.

Maps, diagrams and images

The status

The mapping is false and the method was never validated. The useful residue is the idea that different brain regions have different functions — localisation — which later work established with clinical and imaging evidence, not with skulls.

What is established

Documented: the practice.

Not supported: the skull mapping.

Survives: the idea of localisation.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the classic case of a wrong method producing a right direction.
