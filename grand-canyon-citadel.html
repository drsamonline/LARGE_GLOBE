The Grand Canyon Citadel

A newspaper story about an ancient citadel in the Grand Canyon, with Egyptian and Tibetan artefacts. It was a hoax.

The story

In 1909 a newspaper reported that an explorer had found a cave in the Grand Canyon containing an ancient citadel, mummies and artefacts from Egypt and Tibet. The story was repeated in books about lost civilisations.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The report appeared in a single newspaper and was not corroborated; the explorer's identity and credentials do not hold up, and the Smithsonian has stated that it has no record of the find. No such cave has been located, and the geology of the canyon does not support the described structure.

The archive files it as debunked because the story's source is one newspaper article.

What is established

Documented: the newspaper report.

Documented: the absence of corroboration.

Documented: the Smithsonian's statement.

Not found: any such site.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the classic newspaper-origin legend, and the paper is the only evidence.
