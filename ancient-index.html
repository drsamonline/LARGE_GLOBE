The Ancient 200

Two hundred ancient-world theories — sunken continents, pyramid engineering, sky visitors, out-of-place artefacts and the archaeology that has overturned the textbooks — each labelled with what the evidence says.

How to read this index

The third of the collection indexes, after The 200 and The Modern 200. One line per claim, the archive's evidence label, and a link where a dossier goes deeper. This list is unusual in one way: the majority of its entries are genuine scholarly arguments, so the interesting distinction is not real versus fake but settled versus still open.

48Debunked

The labels: Documented — the thing exists or happened, on the record. Live debate — real evidence, real specialists, no settled answer. Fringe — no positive evidence, often unfalsifiable in practice. Debunked — tested and failed, or a known hoax. Fiction — invented story, legend or film plot, labelled as such rather than as a claim about the world.

What this collection gets wrong

Most of these entries are fair summaries. Eleven are not, and the index marks them rather than repeating the framing:

  • Atlantis in Antarctica needs a crustal shift that does not happen, and ice cores show the Antarctic sheet has been in place for tens of millions of years.
  • Mu and Kumari Kandam have no sunken continent behind them: the shelves are drowned land, but no cities, no population, no continent.
  • The Mohenjo-daro "atomic blast" is vitrified brick from kilns and slag, and the "radioactive skeletons" were never measured.
  • Gunung Padang: the 2023 pyramid paper was disputed by geologists and has been retracted; the hill is a terraced site, not a 20,000-year-old building.
  • The Ica stones were carved by locals who demonstrated it, and the Acámbaro figurines came from paid diggers working for one collector.
  • The Kensington Runestone and Bat Creek stone are 19th-century fabrications; the runes are modern Swedish and the "Hebrew" is not Hebrew.
  • The Grand Canyon citadel appears in one 1909 newspaper article and in no Smithsonian record, excavation report or catalogue.
  • Pakal's lid is a maize-and-underworld scene; the "rocket" reading depends on ignoring the tree at its centre.
  • Tutankhamun's hidden chambers: repeated radar and neutron scans between 2015 and 2020 found no doorways.
  • Salting Carthage has no ancient source, and Nero fiddling is doubly impossible — the violin did not exist and he was at Antium.
  • The Library of Alexandria was not destroyed in a single fire; it decayed over centuries of funding cuts and civil war.

Two entries also deserve promotion rather than correction: Homo floresiensis is no longer disputed as a species, and Zealandia was formally described as a continent in 2017.
