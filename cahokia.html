Cahokia's Emptying

The largest pre-Columbian city north of Mexico, abandoned within two centuries. Its population and its end are both argued.

The city

Cahokia, in Illinois, was the largest settlement in pre-Columbian North America, with earthen mounds including Monks Mound, a planned plaza, and a population estimated in the tens of thousands at its peak around 1100.

Maps, diagrams and images

The end

By around 1350 the city was largely empty. Proposed causes include drought, flooding, resource depletion, disease and political collapse. The evidence points to a combination, and the site's significance is documented in archaeology.

The archive files it as live debate because the abandonment is documented and the explanation is not.
