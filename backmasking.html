Backmasking

The claim that rock records contain hidden messages played backwards. The messages are found by the listener, not put there.

The claim

From the 1960s and especially the 1980s, claims circulated that records contained satanic messages audible when played backwards, leading to legislative proposals and record-burning.

The evidence

Backmasking is a real studio technique, and some artists have deliberately used it. The claimed messages are usually found by listeners primed with the expected phrase — an example of auditory pareidolia — and studies have shown that people hear what they are told to hear.

What is established

Documented: the technique.

Documented: deliberate uses.

Not established: hidden satanic messages.

Explained: by pareidolia.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a claim that was tested with a tape machine and a room of listeners.
