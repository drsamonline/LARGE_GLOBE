The Efficiency Wage

Paying above the market rate can raise productivity. The theory is documented; the size is argued.

The theory

Efficiency-wage theory holds that paying above market rates can raise productivity by reducing turnover, attracting better applicants, improving morale and making dismissal costly. It explains why firms sometimes do not cut wages in a slump.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Field experiments, including a well-known study of fruit pickers, have found productivity gains from wage increases; the size varies and some studies find no effect. The theory is documented and its magnitude is contested.

Where it stands

Why it is in the archive

Because it is an economic claim that has been tested in the field, which is rarer than it should be.
