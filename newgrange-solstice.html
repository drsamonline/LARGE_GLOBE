Newgrange and the Solstice

A passage tomb in Ireland aligned so that the rising sun at midwinter reaches the chamber floor. The alignment is measured every year.

The monument

Newgrange is a passage tomb in the Boyne Valley, built around 3200 BC, with a long passage and a chamber under a mound. Above the entrance is a roof box, an opening designed to admit light.

The alignment

For several days around the winter solstice, the rising sun passes through the roof box and along the passage, reaching the chamber floor. The alignment is precise enough that it cannot be accidental, and it is observed each year by visitors selected by lottery.

The monument is older than Stonehenge and the pyramids, and it is documented in excavation reports and in the annual observations.

What is established

Documented: the monument and its date.

Documented: the roof box and the alignment.

Documented: the annual observation.

Contested: the ritual meaning.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real astronomical alignment that anyone can watch, which is the answer to a shelf of claims about ancient astronomy.
