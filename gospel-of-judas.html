The Gospel of Judas

A Gnostic gospel in which Judas is asked to betray Jesus. The manuscript is real, and it was published and then conserved.

The text

The Gospel of Judas is a Coptic text from a codex found in Egypt in the 1970s and published in 2006. It presents Judas as acting on Jesus's instruction, which reverses the canonical account, and it belongs to the Gnostic tradition.

Maps, diagrams and images

The manuscript

The codex was radiocarbon dated to around 280 AD, and its contents were reconstructed from fragments that had deteriorated badly. Its publication was accompanied by conservation work, and its text is now in the scholarly record.

The archive files it as documented because the manuscript and its dating are established.

What is established

Documented: the manuscript and its date.

Documented: the text's content.

Documented: its Gnostic character.

Contested: its relation to earlier traditions.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real manuscript that changes what the tradition looked like, not what happened.
