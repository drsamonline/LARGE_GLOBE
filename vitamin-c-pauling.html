Vitamin C and Linus Pauling

A double Nobel laureate claimed high-dose vitamin C treats cancer and colds. The trials did not agree.

The claim

Linus Pauling, a Nobel laureate in chemistry and peace, argued from the 1970s that high doses of vitamin C prevent colds and treat cancer, based on his own reading of the literature and on a collaborator's uncontrolled studies.

Maps, diagrams and images

The trials

Randomised trials found at most a small reduction in cold duration and no benefit in cancer treatment. Pauling disputed the trials' design and maintained his position until his death; the evidence has not changed in his favour.

What is established

Documented: the claim and its author.

Documented: the trials.

Not supported: the cancer claim.

Small: any cold effect.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because authority is not evidence, and this is the clearest case of a great scientist being wrong in public.
