The Brahmastra

The Mahabharata describes celestial weapons of enormous destructive power. They have been read as nuclear bombs. They are literary devices in a poem about dharma.

The text

The Mahabharata and the Ramayana describe astras — weapons invoked with a mantra and associated with a deity — including the brahmastra, said to be capable of destroying the world. The descriptions include blinding light, thunder, and devastation of the land.

Maps, diagrams and images

The epics are vast poetic works composed and transmitted over centuries, in a genre where scale is a literary instrument: heroes are larger, battles are bigger, and weapons are cosmic.

The claim

The nuclear reading points to the light, the blast and the aftermath, and to passages about land that is barren and water that is poisoned. It requires reading hyperbole as technical description, and it requires ignoring the rest of the epic's imagery, which is equally extreme and equally symbolic.

There is no archaeological, radiological or textual evidence of a nuclear event in the relevant period, and the dating of the epics is centuries wide.

What is established

Documented: the texts, their genre, and their composition history.

Documented: the astras as divine weapons in a literary cosmology.

Not established: any nuclear event, or any technology.

The error: reading a poetic convention as a technical report.

The label: fiction.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most popular ancient-technology claim in India after the Iron Pillar, and because the answer is the genre, not the physics.
