Asbestos and Radon Cover-Ups

Both asbestos and radon were known hazards long before they were regulated, and in both cases industry documents show the knowledge preceded the action.

Asbestos

The health effects of asbestos were documented in the early twentieth century, and industry-funded research in the 1930s–1960s confirmed the link to asbestosis and mesothelioma while companies suppressed and contested it. Internal documents, disclosed through litigation, show that executives discussed the risks and chose not to warn workers.

Radon

Radon, a radioactive gas from uranium decay, accumulates in buildings and is a leading cause of lung cancer among non-smokers. Its risks were established in the 1950s–1980s through uranium miner studies, and regulation of domestic exposure followed slowly, with industry and some governments resisting remediation costs.

Both cases follow the same pattern: the hazard is established, the evidence is contested for decades, and the regulation arrives after the harm.

What is established

Documented: the hazards, and the dates of the evidence.

Documented: the internal industry documents on asbestos.

Documented: the delayed regulation of both.

Contested: the extent of deliberate concealment in each case.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because these are the clearest cases of a real cover-up that killed people slowly, and because the documents are now public.
