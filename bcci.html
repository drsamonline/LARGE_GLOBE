BCCI

A bank grew into a global institution by serving dictators, arms dealers and intelligence services, and was closed by regulators in 1991. It is the largest bank fraud prosecuted to that date.

The bank

Bank of Credit and Commerce International was founded in 1972 by Agha Hasan Abedi, with capital from the Gulf and a structure of holding companies that spread its operations across dozens of jurisdictions. It grew quickly by serving customers that mainstream banks avoided: regimes under sanctions, arms traders, drug traffickers and intelligence agencies.

Maps, diagrams and images

The collapse

Regulators in several countries moved against it in 1991, and it was closed with losses of around $10 billion, much of it from depositors' accounts. Investigations in the US and UK found systematic fraud, false accounting and a deliberate structure designed to frustrate supervision.

Its customers included the CIA and other intelligence services, which had used it as a channel — a fact that complicated the investigations and feeds the conspiracy literature.

What is established

Documented: the fraud, the structure and the closure.

Documented: the scale of the losses.

Documented: the intelligence use of the bank.

Contested: how much of the intelligence use was known to regulators.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where the conspiracy literature's instincts were right — the bank was a criminal enterprise — and the reality is more bureaucratic than the myth.
