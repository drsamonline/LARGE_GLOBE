Nan Madol

A city built on artificial islets from stacked basalt columns, in a lagoon in Micronesia. It is real, and it took centuries.

The site

Nan Madol lies off Pohnpei in the Federated States of Micronesia: around 90 artificial islets built in a lagoon, with walls and platforms of stacked basalt columns, constructed between roughly the twelfth and seventeenth centuries by the Saudeleur dynasty.

Maps, diagrams and images

The achievement

The columns were quarried elsewhere on Pohnpei and moved by raft and canoe, then stacked without mortar into walls up to several metres high. The labour and logistics are the extraordinary part, and they are documented in archaeology and in Pohnpei's oral tradition.

The site is often cited in claims about lost civilisations; the documented account is a Pacific society with organisation and boats.
