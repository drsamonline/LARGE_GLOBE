The Labyrinth of Hawara

A vast mortuary complex described by Herodotus and Strabo, of which only fragments survive.

The accounts

Herodotus and Strabo described a vast complex of courtyards and chambers at Hawara in Egypt, attached to the pyramid of Amenemhat III. Herodotus claimed it surpassed the pyramids; Strabo counted the rooms.

Maps, diagrams and images

The remains

Excavation has found the pyramid, parts of the mortuary temple and a Roman-era cemetery, but not the structure described. The site has been quarried and eroded for millennia, and the accounts may exaggerate or describe a building already partly ruined.

What is established

Documented: the ancient descriptions.

Documented: the surviving remains.

Not found: the described labyrinth.

Explained: by later destruction.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a wonder that existed and was quarried away, which is a different story from one that never existed.
