The Illuminati

A Bavarian enlightenment society, banned in 1785, has become the supposed secret government of the world. The real organisation lasted nine years and left a paper trail.

The society

The Order of the Illuminati was founded in Bavaria in 1776 by Adam Weishaupt, a professor of canon law, as an Enlightenment secret society opposed to religious and political obscurantism. It recruited officials and intellectuals, and it was banned by the Bavarian authorities in 1785. Its internal papers were seized and published.

Maps, diagrams and images

The claim

The modern claim is that the Illuminati survived, went underground, and now controls governments, banks and media. It rests on eighteenth-century anti-Enlightenment polemic — notably the claim that the society caused the French Revolution — and on a set of symbols, including the eye in the pyramid, which comes from Christian iconography and appears on the US dollar bill for unrelated reasons.

There is no documentary evidence of a surviving organisation. The society's own records end with its suppression.

Where it stands

Documented: the society, its founder, its membership and its suppression.

Documented: the publication of its seized papers.

Not established: any survival, or any current control.

The symbol: older than the society and unrelated to it.

The honest label: fringe.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most-named secret society in the world and the least documented after 1785, and because the paper trail is the answer.
