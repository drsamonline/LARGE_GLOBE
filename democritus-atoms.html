Democritus and Atoms

A Greek philosopher argued that matter consists of indivisible particles. He had no evidence, and he was right.

The argument

Democritus and his teacher Leucippus argued that matter consists of indivisible particles moving in void, and that their arrangements produce the properties we perceive. The argument was philosophical: change requires something unchanging, and divisibility cannot continue forever.

Maps, diagrams and images

The status

The idea survived through Epicurus and Lucretius and was rejected by Aristotle, whose authority dominated. It was revived in the seventeenth century and confirmed experimentally in the twentieth. Democritus had no evidence and the right answer.

The archive files it as documented because the texts and the later confirmation are both real.
