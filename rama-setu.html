Rama Setu

A chain of shoals between India and Sri Lanka, sacred in one account and a natural feature in another.

The feature

Adam's Bridge, or Rama Setu, is a chain of limestone shoals between Rameswaram and Mannar. Geological surveys describe it as a natural formation of sand and limestone; the Ramayana describes a bridge built by an army.

Maps, diagrams and images

The argument

The feature is documented and its natural origin is the geological position. Claims that it is artificial are not supported by survey data, and the dispute has been legal and political as well as scientific.

Where it stands

Documented: the shoals.

Documented: geological surveys.

Not supported: an artificial origin.

Contested: legally and politically.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a natural feature with a sacred account, and the two are not in conflict unless someone insists they are.

What the geology says, and what it does not settle

The chain of shoals between India and Sri Lanka is a real feature with a real history: it was a continuous land connection during periods of lower sea level, which is why the two landmasses share fauna and why the crossing was possible long before any text describes it.

The geological fact does not decide the textual question. A natural causeway does not make the story false, and a story about a causeway does not make the feature artificial. The archive keeps the page because the argument usually presented is that the feature's existence confirms the narrative, and existence is the one thing that proves nothing on its own.
