Orch-OR

The claim that consciousness arises from quantum computation in microtubules inside neurons. It has a testable prediction, one experiment that claimed to support it, and no replication.

The proposal

Orchestrated objective reduction, proposed by Roger Penrose and Stuart Hameroff in 1994, holds that consciousness arises from quantum state reductions in microtubules — protein structures inside neurons — rather than from neural network activity. The 'orchestration' is the biological part; the 'objective reduction' is Penrose's proposed quantum-gravity mechanism.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The decoherence objection is the main one: quantum states in warm, wet tissue collapse in around 10⁻²⁰ seconds, far too fast for the millisecond timescales of neural processing. The theory's answer is that microtubules provide shielding, which is a claim rather than a demonstration.

A 2022 experiment reported anaesthetic effects on microtubules in rats that were interpreted as support; it has not been replicated independently, and the interpretation has been disputed.

Where it stands

Documented: the theory, and its specific predictions.

Contested: the decoherence objection and the shielding answer.

Unreplicated: the 2022 anaesthetic result.

Not established: any quantum role in consciousness.

The honest label: fringe — a serious, specific, unevidenced proposal.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most respectable quantum-consciousness theory, and because its specific predictions make it more falsifiable than most of the genre.
