The Ninth Legion

A Roman legion stationed in Britain disappears from the record, and has been sent to Scotland, annihilated, or transferred. The evidence points to the dull answer.

The unit

Legio IX Hispana was a Roman legion with a long history, stationed in Britain in the first century AD. Its last securely dated record in Britain is around 120 AD; after that it disappears from the epigraphic record.

Maps, diagrams and images

The explanations

The romantic explanation is destruction in Scotland, and it has produced novels and films. The evidence points elsewhere: a building inscription from Nijmegen dated to about 121 AD refers to the legion, suggesting it was transferred to the continent. A tile stamp from the same region supports a posting there.

Its later fate is unknown, but the 'annihilated in Scotland' story is not supported by any find, and the transfer explanation fits the epigraphy.

Where it stands

Documented: the legion, its British service and its records.

Documented: the Nijmegen inscription of c. 121 AD.

Not supported: destruction in Scotland.

Unknown: the legion's final disposition.

The honest label: live debate, leaning to transfer.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best-known 'lost legion', and because the answer is an administrative record in the Netherlands.
