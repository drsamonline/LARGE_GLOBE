The Sailing Stones

Rocks on a dry lake bed leave long tracks, apparently moving on their own. The explanation — ice sheets pushed by light wind — was confirmed with GPS and time-lapse in 2014.

The phenomenon

On the Racetrack Playa in Death Valley, stones leave trails in the dry mud, some tens of metres long. Nobody had seen them move, and the trails persist for years. The phenomenon was documented from 1915 and investigated for decades.

Maps, diagrams and images

The solution

In 2014 Richard Norris and colleagues instrumented the playa with GPS-tracked rocks and time-lapse cameras. They recorded movement in winter, when a thin layer of water freezes at night into ice sheets; as the ice breaks up and a light wind pushes the floating sheets, the rocks are dragged along, leaving the trails.

The movement is slow — a few metres per minute at most — and rare, which is why nobody saw it for a century.

What is established

Documented: the trails, over a century.

Documented: the mechanism, filmed and measured in 2014.

Required conditions: water, freezing nights, light wind.

Not mysterious: the phenomenon is solved.

The label: documented — and one of the few on this shelf with a complete answer.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best case of a mystery that was solved by instrumenting it, and because the solution is more interesting than the folklore.
