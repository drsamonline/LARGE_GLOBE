Navier–Stokes Existence and Smoothness

The equations that describe fluid flow are used every day and have never been proved to have solutions that stay smooth. Turbulence is the reason it matters.

The problem

The Navier–Stokes equations describe the motion of viscous fluids. In three dimensions, nobody has proved that smooth solutions exist for all time from smooth initial data, or that they can blow up in finite time. It is one of the Clay Millennium Prize problems.

Why it is hard

The difficulty is the nonlinearity: the equations contain a term that can concentrate energy at small scales, and whether it can concentrate enough to produce a singularity is unknown. Turbulence — the disorderly, multiscale motion seen in every real flow — is the physical face of the problem.

Engineers and physicists proceed regardless, using numerical methods that work well in practice. That gap between practical success and mathematical proof is the point.

Where it stands

Used: everywhere, in engineering and physics.

Solved: numerically, in practice.

Open: existence and smoothness in 3D.

Known: results in two dimensions.

The label: open problem.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the clearest case of a theory that works perfectly well without the proof, which is a useful thing for a reader of this archive to understand.
