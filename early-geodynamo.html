The Early Geodynamo

The Earth's magnetic field is generated by a dynamo in the core, and it was running more than three billion years ago. How, with a hotter core, is the problem.

The evidence

Palaeomagnetic measurements of ancient rocks, and of tiny magnetic inclusions in zircon crystals, indicate that a magnetic field existed more than three billion years ago, and possibly as early as 4.2 billion years ago. That is far earlier than simple thermal convection in the core would predict.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problem

A hotter early Earth means a hotter core, with higher thermal conductivity, which makes it harder to sustain a dynamo by thermal convection alone. Proposed solutions include compositional convection driven by the crystallisation of the inner core — which may have started later — exsolution of light elements, and a basal magma ocean.

The evidence for an early field is strong; the mechanism is argued.

Where it stands

Documented: palaeomagnetic evidence for an early field.

Contested: the power source.

Problem: a hot core is a poor dynamo.

Proposals: compositional convection; exsolution.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the observation is clear and the mechanism is missing, which is the ordinary state of geophysics.
