Atlantis at Doñana

A team argued that Atlantis lies under the marshes of Doñana in southern Spain, destroyed by a tsunami. The site has structures, and the identification is not accepted.

The claim

In 2004 a German researcher, Rainer Kühne, and later a team including Richard Freund argued that Atlantis was located in the marshes of Doñana, near Cádiz, and that the concentric rings described by Plato could be seen in satellite imagery. A 2011 National Geographic documentary presented the case, and a tsunami was proposed as the destroyer.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The 'rings' are consistent with natural and later human features in a wetland, and the structures found by geophysical survey have not been dated to the period required or shown to be a city. Spanish archaeologists working in the area have rejected the identification, and no peer-reviewed case has established it.

The wider problem is the same as with every Atlantis location: the Plato text is a philosophical dialogue, and the search for its setting has produced dozens of candidates, each with a local feature that fits part of the description.

Where it stands

Documented: the claim, the imagery and the survey work.

Not established: the identification with Atlantis.

Contested: the dating and character of the structures.

Rejected: by most archaeologists working in the region.

The honest label: live debate, leaning against.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best-argued Atlantis location, and because it shows how a real landscape with real features can absorb a legend.
