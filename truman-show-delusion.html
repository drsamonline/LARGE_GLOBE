The Truman Show Delusion

A delusion in which the patient believes their life is a television show and everyone around them is acting. It is documented, it is rare, and it is named after a film.

The phenomenon

In 2008 the psychiatrists Joel and Ian Gold described a small series of patients who believed their lives were being filmed and broadcast, with the people around them playing roles. The content closely followed the plot of The Truman Show, and the Golds named the presentation after it.

What it is

It is not a new disease. The patients had diagnoses including schizophrenia and bipolar disorder, and the delusion is a culturally shaped version of a familiar persecutory belief: that one is being watched, that events are staged, that others are actors. Delusion content has always tracked the available technology — radio in the 1920s, television later, cameras and the internet now.

The value of the description is in what it shows about how delusions take their content from the culture, not in any new mechanism.

What is established

Documented: the case series and the description.

Documented: the underlying diagnoses.

Documented: the cultural shaping of delusion content.

Not established: any new syndrome.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it shows the mechanism behind a whole class of claims here: a belief that is shaped by the media available to the believer.
