The Los Lunas Stone

A boulder in New Mexico inscribed with the Ten Commandments in Hebrew, said to be ancient. The inscription is modern.

The object

The Los Lunas Decalogue Stone is a large boulder near Los Lunas, New Mexico, inscribed with a version of the Ten Commandments in a Hebrew script. It has been cited as evidence of ancient transatlantic contact.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The script is a form used in modern printed Hebrew, and the inscription includes a carving of a figure that is not part of the Decalogue. The earliest references to it are from the 1930s, and the site has no archaeological context. It is widely treated as a modern inscription.

The archive files it as debunked because the script dates the object.

What is established

Documented: the stone and the inscription.

Documented: the script's modern form.

Documented: the first references in the 1930s.

Not established: any ancient origin.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a clean case where palaeography settles a claim that archaeology could not.
