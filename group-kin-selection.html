Group versus Kin Selection

Why do animals help each other? Hamilton's rule explains a lot, and the argument about group selection has never quite ended.

The positions

Kin selection explains altruism through relatedness: a gene can spread by helping relatives. Group selection proposes that traits can spread because groups with them outcompete groups without. Hamilton's rule formalised the first and appeared to settle the argument.

Maps, diagrams and images

The revival

Multilevel selection theory revived group selection in a mathematically respectable form, and the debate since has been about whether the two frameworks are different theories or the same one in different notation. Most biologists use inclusive fitness; the argument is about emphasis and about cultural evolution.

The archive files it as live debate.

Where it stands

Documented: Hamilton's rule and its successes.

Documented: multilevel selection theory.

Contested: whether they differ.

Contested: the scope for culture.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a genuine scientific argument that outsiders routinely mistake for a settled one.
