Where to Read More

Every topic in this archive has a serious literature behind it. These are the places that hold it — chosen because they publish sources, not because they agree with us.

Use this page instead of a search engine. Searching “hollow earth” gives you a hundred videos and a wiki; searching inside a geological survey gives you the paper. All links below are to the front page of the institution, so they stay working when a page is reorganised.

Fact-checking and claim testing

  • Snopes — snopes.com — the oldest general fact-checking archive; strong on viral claims and their provenance.
  • Reuters Fact Check — reuters.com/fact-check — the source used repeatedly in this archive for Antarctic and map claims.
  • AP Fact Check — apnews.com/hub/ap-fact-check
  • Full Fact (UK) — fullfact.org
  • Skeptoid — skeptoid.com — short, sourced episodes; the Tartaria and hollow-Earth episodes are on the reading lists.
  • Google Fact Check Explorer — toolbox.google.com/factcheck/explorer
  • Retraction Watch — retractionwatch.com — for checking whether a paper still stands.

Space, stars and the sky

  • NASA — nasa.gov — mission archives, the Tunguska and Apollo records, and the image library.
  • ESA — esa.int — Gaia, which produced the 1.3-million binary star catalogue.
  • JPL — jpl.nasa.gov — the Earth-radius study cited in the larger-globe dossier.
  • NASA Image and Video Library — images.nasa.gov — public domain imagery.
  • IAU — iau.org — the body that actually names things.

Earth, rocks and deep time

  • USGS — usgs.gov — the source of the plate-tectonics and dinosaur-timing statements here.
  • British Geological Survey — bgs.ac.uk
  • Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program — volcano.si.edu
  • IRIS / EarthScope — iris.edu — how seismic waves image the interior, the test that rules out a hollow planet.
  • National Park Service — nps.gov — Devils Tower’s own geology pages.
  • IUGS Geoheritage — iugs-geoheritage.org

Oceans and polar regions

  • NOAA Ocean Exploration — oceanexplorer.noaa.gov
  • NOAA NCEI — ncei.noaa.gov — Seabed 2030 and the mapping statistics.
  • Schmidt Ocean Institute — schmidtocean.org — live deep-sea expeditions.
  • MBARI — mbari.org — deep-sea biology and video.
  • NSIDC — nsidc.org — sea ice and polar data.
  • British Antarctic Survey — bas.ac.uk
  • Byrd Polar and Climate Research Center — byrd.osu.edu — Byrd’s actual papers.

Archaeology, museums and the ancient world

  • Penn Museum — penn.museum — Expedition magazine, including the Palenque analysis.
  • British Museum — britishmuseum.org — collection database and research publications.
  • The Met, Open Access — metmuseum.org — free images of half a million objects.
  • Archaeological Institute of America — archaeological.org — and its magazine, Archaeology.
  • Antiquity — antiquity.ac.uk — peer-reviewed archaeology, often open access.
  • Open Context — opencontext.org — raw excavation data.

Myth, folklore and primary texts

  • Theoi Project — theoi.com — Greek myth with the classical citations attached.
  • Perseus Digital Library — perseus.tufts.edu — Greek and Latin texts with translations.
  • Internet Sacred Text Archive — sacred-texts.com — public-domain scriptures and folklore.
  • Project Gutenberg — gutenberg.org
  • The Folklore Society — folklore-society.com
  • Library of Congress research guides — guides.loc.gov — including the hollow-earth bibliography.

Secret programmes and declassified files

  • CIA FOIA Reading Room — cia.gov/readingroom
  • FBI Records: The Vault — vault.fbi.gov
  • NSA FOIA — nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA
  • National Archives (US) — archives.gov
  • GovInfo — govinfo.gov — congressional hearings and reports.
  • AARO — aaro.mil — the current US UAP office.

Images you can legally reuse

  • Wikimedia Commons — commons.wikimedia.org — every photograph on this site, with author and licence recorded on the credits page.
  • Library of Congress — loc.gov
  • Rijksmuseum — rijksmuseum.nl
  • Wellcome Collection — wellcomecollection.org — history of medicine and science.
  • David Rumsey Map Collection — davidrumsey.com — the maps that “Tartary” claims are built on.

Archives and libraries (the useful deep web)

  • Internet Archive — archive.org — Wayback Machine, texts, audio.
  • HathiTrust — hathitrust.org — 18 million scanned volumes.
  • WorldCat — worldcat.org — find the physical book.
  • arXiv — arxiv.org · PubMed Central — pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov · DOAJ — doaj.org
  • CORE — core.ac.uk — open-access papers aggregated.
How to use a directory like this

Pick the institution that would be embarrassed by the claim. If the claim is geological, check the geological survey. If it is about a manuscript, check the library that holds it. If it is about a secret programme, check the agency’s own FOIA reading room — because that is where the documents that proved six real conspiracies actually live.

Also worth bookmarking

  • Wikipedia — useful as a map of a topic and a list of its citations, not as an endpointen.wikipedia.org
  • Internet Archive Scholarscholar.archive.org
  • Google Books — for checking what a 19th-century source actually saidbooks.google.com
