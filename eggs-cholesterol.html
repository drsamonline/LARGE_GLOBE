Eggs and Cholesterol

Dietary cholesterol was said to raise blood cholesterol and heart risk. The advice has been revised, and the debate continues.

The advice

From the 1970s, dietary guidelines advised limiting eggs and other cholesterol-rich foods to reduce heart disease. The advice shaped school meals and public health messaging for decades.

The revision

Later research found that dietary cholesterol has a modest effect on blood cholesterol in most people, and that saturated fat matters more. Several guidelines dropped specific limits on eggs, while keeping advice for people with diabetes or existing heart disease. The debate continues over individual variation.

Where it stands

Documented: the original advice.

Documented: the revision.

Modest: the dietary effect.

Contested: individual variation.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the official advice changed, which is what should happen and rarely gets recorded.
