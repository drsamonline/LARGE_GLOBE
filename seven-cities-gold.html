The Seven Cities of Gold

Spanish explorers were told of seven golden cities to the north. Coronado found pueblos of stone and mud, and no gold.

The legend

The legend of the Seven Cities — Cíbola — reached the Spanish from Indigenous accounts and from a survivor of an earlier expedition, Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca, who reported large settlements to the north. A Franciscan, Marcos de Niza, returned claiming to have seen a wealthy city.

Maps, diagrams and images

The expedition

In 1540 Francisco Vázquez de Coronado led an expedition north with several hundred men. They reached Zuni pueblos in what is now New Mexico: substantial stone and adobe settlements, but without the gold the accounts promised. The expedition continued east and north for two years and returned bankrupt.

The pueblos were real cities. The gold was not there, and the descriptions had grown in the retelling.

What is established

Documented: the legend, the accounts and the expedition.

Documented: the Zuni pueblos and their wealth in food, not gold.

Not found: any city of gold.

Explained: the growth of the account in transmission.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the same error as El Dorado in a different direction: a real settlement described by someone who wanted it to be rich.
