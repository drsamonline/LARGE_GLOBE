Cambridge Analytica

A political consulting firm obtained Facebook data on millions of users through a personality quiz app, and used it for targeted advertising. The scandal changed how the platform handled data.

The harvesting

In 2014 a researcher at Cambridge University built a Facebook quiz app that collected data not only from users who installed it but from their friends — a feature the platform then allowed. The data covered tens of millions of profiles and was passed to Cambridge Analytica, a political consulting firm.

The exposure

The arrangement was reported in 2018 by the Guardian and the New York Times, with testimony from a former employee. Facebook suspended the firm, apologised, and later paid penalties in the US and UK; the firm collapsed. Investigations found the platform's data controls had been inadequate and its disclosures misleading.

What remains contested is the effect: the firm claimed its 'psychographic' targeting was decisive, and the evidence for that claim is weak.

What is established

Documented: the data harvesting, its scale and the transfer.

Documented: the platform's inadequate controls.

Documented: the penalties and the firm's collapse.

Contested: the effectiveness of the targeting.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real scandal whose most dramatic claim — that it swung an election — is the part with the least evidence.
