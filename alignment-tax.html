The Alignment Tax

Safety measures are said to cost capability. The size of the cost is argued and measured differently.

The claim

The alignment tax is the loss in capability or efficiency that comes from safety training, refusal behaviour or interpretability constraints. It is used in arguments about how much safety the field can afford.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Measurements show costs in some settings and small or negative costs in others; safety training can improve usefulness, and some constraints cost compute rather than quality. The trade-off is documented; the size depends on the task and the measure.

Where it stands

Documented: the concept.

Measured: in some settings.

Contested: the size.

Sometimes: no cost at all.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a policy argument resting on an empirical claim that is rarely measured in public.
