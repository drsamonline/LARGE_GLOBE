Glossary

The words this archive uses in a specific sense, and the ones that arrive loaded. Definitions are short because they are working definitions: enough to read a page with, not enough to argue from.

Three of these groups are worth separating. The geology terms are the ones fringe writing borrows and redefines — firmament, aether, ice wall — and knowing the technical meaning is most of the answer. The method terms are the ones that decide how a page is labelled. The mind terms are where careful philosophy is most often quoted as if it were physics.

Earth, geology and the sky

Aether
The medium nineteenth-century physics assumed light travelled through. Michelson and Morley failed to detect it in 1887, and relativity removed the need for it. It survives in fringe writing as a catch-all for whatever the vacuum is supposedly made of.
Cap carbonate
A dolostone or limestone laid down directly on glacial deposits. The pairing is the fingerprint of Snowball Earth: ice, then a sudden carbonate-saturated ocean as CO₂ built up and the ice melted. Snowball Earth
Chicxulub
The buried impact crater under the Yucatán, about 180 km across, dated to the Cretaceous–Palaeogene boundary. The dinosaur extinction
Concave Earth
The claim that we live on the inside of a hollow sphere, with the sky at the centre. Geometrically it can be made to fit some observations and fails others, chiefly the behaviour of the horizon and of long-distance radio. The Larger Globe
Cryogenian
The geological period roughly 720–635 million years ago, containing the Sturtian and Marinoan glaciations that Snowball Earth is about.
Expanding Earth
The hypothesis that the planet's radius has grown over geological time, once used to explain the jigsaw fit of the continents. Plate tectonics explains the same fit without expansion, and laser ranging measures the radius as stable to millimetres per year. The Larger Globe
Firmament
The dome or vault of Genesis 1, read literally by flat-earth and some creationist writing as a solid structure above the atmosphere.
Hollow Earth
The claim that the planet is a shell with a habitable interior. Proposed seriously in 1692 by Edmond Halley to explain magnetic variation; ruled out by seismology, which shows the interior is solid and liquid rock and metal, not air. Hollow Earth
Ice wall
The rim of the flat-earth disc, identified with Antarctica. The claim requires every Antarctic expedition, satellite and treaty to be part of the concealment. Beyond the Ice Wall
Iridium anomaly
A thin worldwide layer of iridium at the K–Pg boundary, tens of times the crustal abundance. Iridium is common in meteorites, and the anomaly is the original evidence for an impact.
Inner sun
A light source inside a hollow or concave Earth. It has no mechanism that survives the thermodynamics: a body that luminous inside a shell would cook the shell.
Palaeomagnetism
The record of the Earth's magnetic field frozen into rocks as they form. It is what places Neoproterozoic glacial deposits near the equator, and what first showed the continents had moved.
Plate tectonics
The theory that the lithosphere is broken into plates that move over the asthenosphere. Accepted since the 1960s; it is the reason continental drift, once ridiculed, is now textbook.
Tartarus
In Hesiod, the pit below Hades, as far beneath the earth as the sky is above it. Modern fringe writing relocates it inside the planet. Tartarus
Tillite
Lithified glacial till: unsorted rock debris deposited by ice. Finding one at a palaeolatitude near the equator is the evidence that forces Snowball Earth.

Claims, evidence and method

Base rate
How common something is before you look at the specific case. Ignoring it is how a test with a 99% accuracy produces mostly false positives when the thing tested for is rare.
Bayesian prior
The probability you assign before seeing new evidence, updated afterwards. Most disputes about fringe claims are disputes about priors, disguised as disputes about data.
Cargo cult science
Feynman's term for work that has the form of science — apparatus, statistics, publication — without the honesty about what would falsify it.
Effect size
How large an effect is, as opposed to whether it is distinguishable from zero. A statistically significant effect can be far too small to matter. Ego depletion
Falsifiability
A claim is scientific if something could count against it. A claim that explains every possible observation explains nothing.
Gish gallop
Overwhelming an opponent with many weak arguments faster than they can be answered. Common in long-form fringe video, where the response time is unbounded and the audience's is not.
Meta-analysis
Pooling many studies into one estimate. Its quality depends on the studies pooled: pooling twenty underpowered studies produces a confident wrong answer. Growth mindset
Motte-and-bailey
Defending a modest claim (the motte) when the ambitious one (the bailey) is attacked, then returning to the ambitious one when the pressure is off.
Pathological science
Irving Langmuir's term for research where the effect is at the edge of detection, the measured size tracks the expected size, and claims of extraordinary sensitivity are met with excuses rather than data. Cold fusion
p-value
The probability of data at least this extreme if the null hypothesis were true. It is not the probability that the hypothesis is false, and it is not a measure of importance.
Pre-registration
Publishing the hypothesis, sample size and analysis before collecting data. It removes the freedom to choose the analysis after seeing the result.
Regression to the mean
Extreme measurements tend to be followed by less extreme ones. Much of what looks like a treatment working — or a haunting ending — is this.
Replication crisis
The finding, from the 2010s, that a large share of published results in psychology and some biomedical fields do not reproduce at their original effect size.
Steelman
Stating an opponent's argument in its strongest form before answering it. The archive's house rule: a claim is answered as its best proponents state it, not as its worst.

Mind and consciousness

Emergence
The idea that a system can have properties none of its parts have. Contested precisely where it matters: whether experience is one of them.
Free energy
In Friston's formulation, a bound on the surprise a system's sensory states produce. Minimising it is claimed to unify perception, action and self-maintenance. The free energy principle
Hard problem
Chalmers's question: why physical processing is accompanied by experience at all. Dissolving it, or denying it exists, is the main divide in philosophy of mind.
Markov blanket
The statistical boundary separating a system's internal states from the outside world, mediated by sensory and active states. The formal core of the free energy principle.
Panpsychism
The view that experience, or something like it, is a fundamental feature of matter rather than a product of complexity. Panpsychism
Qualia
The felt character of an experience — what red looks like, what pain is like. Used to argue that physical description leaves something out.

Media, machines and the web

AI slop
Machine-generated text and images produced at a volume no one reads, optimised for engagement or search placement rather than for a reader.
Bot
An automated account. The claim that most of the visible web is automated is partly measurable and partly rhetorical; the measurable part is smaller than usually claimed. The Dead Internet
Deepfake
Synthetic audio or video of a real person saying something they did not say. The documented harm is not only that people believe fakes, but that they dismiss real recordings.
Enshittification
Cory Doctorow's term for platforms degrading their own service for users and sellers in order to extract more from both. Enshittification
Echo chamber
An environment where a claim is repeated mainly by people who already accept it, so that repetition is mistaken for corroboration. Echo chambers

Terms are defined for use inside this archive. Where a term has a contested definition in the literature, the page that uses it says which sense is meant.
