Nero and the Fire

Nero was said to have fiddled while Rome burned, and to have started the fire to clear space for his palace. The first is impossible and the second is unlikely.

The fire

In July 64 AD a fire broke out in the Circus Maximus area and burned for days, destroying much of Rome. Tacitus describes the destruction and the reconstruction, and reports that Nero was away at Antium when it began, returning to organise relief.

The accusations

The 'fiddling' story is a later invention: the violin did not exist, and Nero was not in the city. The arson accusation is reported by Tacitus as a rumour, and he notes that Nero blamed the Christians to deflect it — which is the part of the story that is best evidenced.

Modern historians regard accidental origin as most likely, in a city of timber tenements, and note that Nero's rebuilding — wider streets, brick instead of wood — was a sensible response.

What is established

Documented: the fire, its extent and the rebuilding.

Documented: Nero's absence at the start.

Documented: the scapegoating of Christians.

Not supported: the arson and the fiddle.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most durable character assassination in Western history, and because the source says it was a rumour.
