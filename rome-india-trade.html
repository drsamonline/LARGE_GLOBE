Rome Traded with India

Roman coins are found in India, and a Roman-era text describes the monsoon route. The trade was large enough to worry Roman writers.

The evidence

Roman coins, especially of Augustus and Tiberius, are found in large numbers in southern India, and excavations at Muziris and Arikamedu have produced Roman pottery and glass. The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, a first-century navigational text, describes the route and the ports.

Maps, diagrams and images

The scale

Roman writers complained about the outflow of silver to the East, and the monsoon crossing — discovered to be predictable — made the voyage routine. The trade was substantial and is documented by finds on both ends of the route.

The archive files it as documented because the coins, the ports and the text agree.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a globalised trade route that people are surprised to find in the first century.
