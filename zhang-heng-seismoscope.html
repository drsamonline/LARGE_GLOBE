Zhang Heng's Seismoscope

A bronze vessel that detected earthquakes and indicated direction. The instrument is described in a text and its principle is understood.

The instrument

Zhang Heng, an astronomer at the Han court, presented a seismoscope in 132 AD: a bronze vessel with eight dragons holding balls, which dropped into the mouths of toads when an earthquake occurred, indicating direction. The device is described in the Book of the Later Han.

Maps, diagrams and images

The reconstruction

The original is lost and its internal mechanism is not described in detail, so modern reconstructions are hypotheses. What is documented is that the instrument was used and that it reportedly detected a distant earthquake that no one at court had felt.

The archive files it as documented because the text and the record are real, and it flags the mechanism as reconstructed.

What is established

Documented: the instrument's description.

Documented: its reported success.

Not preserved: the original.

Reconstructed: the mechanism.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real early instrument, and because the gap between the record and the reconstruction is instructive.
