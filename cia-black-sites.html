CIA Black Sites

The CIA ran a network of secret prisons in several countries, where detainees were held and subjected to techniques the US government later acknowledged as torture.

The programme

After 2001 the CIA established a network of secret detention facilities — 'black sites' — in several countries, where high-value detainees were held outside normal legal process and subjected to 'enhanced interrogation techniques', including waterboarding, sleep deprivation and confinement in small boxes.

Maps, diagrams and images

The record

The programme was exposed by journalists in 2005, investigated by the European Parliament and by the US Senate, whose 2014 report found the techniques ineffective as well as brutal. The US government acknowledged the programme and the use of techniques that, in the Senate's words and in the judgment of most legal analysts, constituted torture.

Several European states were found to have hosted or facilitated the programme, and cases continue in the European Court of Human Rights.

What is established

Documented: the programme, the sites and the techniques.

Documented: the Senate report and its findings.

Documented: European complicity, in several cases.

Contested: the legal characterisation, in some jurisdictions.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most consequential documented secret programme of the century, and because it was exposed by the ordinary means the archive relies on: documents and reporting.
