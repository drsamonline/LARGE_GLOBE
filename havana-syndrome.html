Havana Syndrome

Diplomats in Havana reported headaches, dizziness and cognitive problems, and some heard a sound. Eight years of investigation have produced two rival explanations and no confirmed cause.

The cases

From late 2016, US and Canadian diplomats in Havana reported sudden onset of headaches, dizziness, nausea, tinnitus and cognitive difficulties, sometimes with a directional sound. Similar reports followed from other posts — China, Cuba, Vienna, and Washington itself — and the condition acquired the name 'Havana syndrome'.

Maps, diagrams and images

Some patients showed measurable abnormalities on balance and eye-movement testing, and a 2024 study reported brain imaging differences in a small cohort. Others showed nothing, and the case definitions varied between studies, which is a large part of the problem.

The two explanations

The directed-energy hypothesis proposes a microwave or acoustic device causing tissue damage. It has been supported by some government-funded studies and by a 2024 study from the University of Pennsylvania, and it has not produced a device, a mechanism, or a dose-response relationship.

The mass psychogenic illness hypothesis proposes that symptoms spread within a stressed community, as they have in documented outbreaks for centuries. A 2021 CIA interim assessment reportedly leaned this way for most cases, and the recorded 'sonic attack' was later matched to the call of a local cricket.

Where it stands

Documented: the cluster of symptoms, and the political consequences.

Documented: some objective findings in subsets of patients.

Contested: whether a directed-energy device exists and was used.

Contested: whether mass psychogenic illness explains most cases.

The honest label: live debate — with the strong caveat that the two explanations are not mutually exclusive, and that no cause has been established.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the symptom is documented, the cause is not, and the interpretation has become a matter of institutional position — which is where the archive's labels earn their keep.
