Akhenaten's Revolution

One pharaoh replaced Egypt's gods with a single sun disc, moved the capital, and changed the art. The revolution is documented; why it happened is not.

The change

Akhenaten, who ruled around 1351–1334 BC, promoted the Aten — the sun disc — above the other gods, built a new capital at Amarna, and encouraged an art style that abandoned the formal conventions of Egyptian representation for something more fluid and less idealised. Hymns to the Aten survive, and they are among the most striking religious texts from Egypt.

Maps, diagrams and images

The questions

Whether this was true monotheism, henotheism, or a political move against the priesthood of Amun is argued. The revolution was reversed within a generation: Tutankhamun restored the old cults, the capital was abandoned, and Akhenaten's name was later erased from monuments.

The evidence is abundant — Amarna was excavated and its archives survive — but the motive is not recorded.

What is established

Documented: the reign, the Aten cult, the capital and the art.

Documented: the reversal under Tutankhamun.

Documented: the erasure of his name.

Contested: the religious and political motive.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the clearest case of a documented religious revolution, and because it shows how much of ancient history is evidence without motive.
