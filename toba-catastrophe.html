The Toba Catastrophe

A supereruption in Sumatra and a theory that it nearly ended humanity. The eruption is documented; the bottleneck is contested.

The eruption

Around 74,000 years ago the Toba caldera in Sumatra produced a supereruption, one of the largest known, with ash deposits found across Asia and a global cooling signal in some records.

Maps, diagrams and images

The bottleneck

The theory holds that the eruption caused a volcanic winter that reduced human populations to a few thousand, producing the genetic bottleneck seen in modern humans. The genetic evidence for a bottleneck is real but its timing and cause are disputed, and archaeological evidence from India and South Africa suggests human populations continued through the period.

The archive files it as live debate: the eruption is documented, the catastrophe for humans is not.

Where it stands

Documented: the eruption and its ash.

Documented: the genetic bottleneck signal.

Contested: the timing and cause.

Contested: the human impact.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real catastrophe attached to an overstated consequence.
