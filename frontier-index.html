The Frontier 200

Two hundred debates where working scientists are actually divided — dark matter's composition, quantum interpretations, the origin of life, consciousness, what killed the megafauna, and whether AI ends work.

How to read an index that is almost all "live debate"

The fourth collection index, after The 200, The Modern 200 and The Ancient 200. These are not fringe claims — they are papers, preprint arguments and conference fights among people with the same data. So the useful question is not "is this true?" but how much weight does each side carry?

172Live debate
17Fringe
4Debunked

The labels: Documented — the phenomenon is on record even where its explanation is not (the Hubble tension exists; time crystals have been built). Live debate — real evidence on both sides, no settled answer. Fringe — a minority position without positive evidence, or one that has failed its own tests. Debunked — tested and failed as a claim. Fiction — invention, labelled as such.

Where this list overstates the split

A list of "sharp divides" tends to flatten real asymmetries. Nine entries here are not evenly balanced, and the index marks them:

  • Cold fusion is not a live scientific debate. The 1989 claim failed replication, and the excess-heat results have never met the standards of the field.
  • Orch-OR has no evidence of quantum coherence in microtubules at body temperature, and decoherence timescales make it implausible.
  • Polyvagal theory is widely used in therapy but its evolutionary anatomy does not survive scrutiny; that is a clinical-practice problem, not a balanced disagreement.
  • The aquatic ape is a fringe position, not a school of anthropology — there is no fossil or comparative evidence for a semi-aquatic phase.
  • Learning styles and multiple intelligences are educationally popular and empirically unsupported; the studies are not split, the marketing is.
  • The Mozart effect produced one small arousal finding in 1993 and has not survived replication as an intelligence effect.
  • The axis of evil has largely been retired as a statistical artefact of the way the sky is cut for analysis.
  • Superdeterminism, the simulation argument, the doomsday argument and the Omega Point are philosophy or mathematics with no empirical handle, which is why they sit in Fringe rather than Debate.
  • The Silurian hypothesis is a thought experiment about detectability, not a claim that a previous civilisation existed.

Two entries are stronger than "debate" implies: Chicxulub is settled as the impact (only its relative role versus the Deccan traps is argued), and Clovis-first has been overturned — the argument now is how much earlier people arrived, not whether they did.
