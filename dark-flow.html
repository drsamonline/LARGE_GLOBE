Dark Flow

A 2008 analysis reported that galaxy clusters are all drifting toward a patch of sky beyond the observable universe. If true it would break the assumption that the universe looks the same in every direction. Later data did not confirm it.

The claim

In 2008 Alexander Kashlinsky and colleagues reported a 'dark flow': after subtracting the expected motion from the cosmic microwave background, clusters of galaxies appeared to share a bulk motion of several hundred kilometres per second toward a region in the sky. If real, the motion would be sourced by matter outside the observable universe, which would violate the assumption of isotropy on the largest scales.

Maps, diagrams and images

The counter-evidence

The result depended on the cluster catalogue and on the subtraction of the microwave background's own motion, and reanalyses found the signal sensitive to both. Planck's data, with better calibration, did not reproduce it; a 2013 analysis using Planck found no significant bulk flow, and subsequent work with larger catalogues has been consistent with the standard cosmological model.

Where it stands

Reported: a bulk flow in 2008, from WMAP-era data.

Not confirmed: by Planck or by later cluster catalogues.

Sensitive to: catalogue selection and the CMB dipole subtraction.

Would require: structure beyond the observable horizon, which is not excluded in principle.

The honest label: live debate that has largely resolved against the original claim.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a rare case of a claim that would have rewritten cosmology, was reported widely, and then quietly failed to replicate — and because the archive should show what that looks like.
