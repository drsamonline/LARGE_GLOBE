Evolutionary Modularity

The claim that the mind is a collection of specialised modules shaped by Pleistocene selection. It is productive and contested, and the evidence for specific modules is thinner than the framework.

The claim

Evolutionary psychology proposes that the mind consists of many domain-specific mechanisms — modules for detecting cheaters, for theory of mind, for language, for fear of snakes — each shaped by selection in ancestral environments. Massive modularity is the strong version: there is no general-purpose processor.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Support comes from dissociations, from developmental evidence for domain-specific learning, and from cross-cultural regularities. The criticisms are that many proposed modules rest on laboratory tasks with alternative explanations, that the Pleistocene environment is unknown in the detail the theory needs, and that neural evidence for the modules is scarce.

The framework has been productive — it generates testable hypotheses — and many of its specific claims have not survived testing.

Where it stands

Supported: domain-specific mechanisms exist for some functions.

Contested: massive modularity.

Contested: most specific module claims.

Productive: as a research framework.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the field most often used to give scientific authority to stories about human nature, and the stories outrun the evidence.
