The Mohenjo-daro Blast

A claim that an ancient city was destroyed by an atomic blast. The 'evidence' is a scattered set of bones and a misread verse.

The claim

The claim holds that Mohenjo-daro was destroyed by a nuclear explosion, citing scattered skeletons, 'vitrified' surfaces and a verse from the Mahabharata describing a weapon of terrible brightness.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The skeletons are from different periods and contexts, and there is no crater, no shocked quartz, no glass of the kind an atomic blast produces, and no radiation anomaly. The Mahabharata verse describes a divine weapon in poetic terms. The city's decline is attributed to the Indus system's changes, including the shifting of rivers.

The archive files it as debunked because the physical evidence that would settle it is absent.

What is established

Documented: the ruins and the skeletons.

Not found: a crater, shocked quartz or blast glass.

Not measured: any radiation anomaly.

Explained: by the city's decline.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most cinematic ancient-war claim, and the evidence for it is a mood.
