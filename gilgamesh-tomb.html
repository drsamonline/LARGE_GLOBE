Gilgamesh's Tomb

A claim that the tomb of Gilgamesh was found under the Euphrates. The structure is real; the identification is a reading.

The claim

In 2003 a German team working at Uruk reported a structure beneath the bed of the Euphrates with a stone chamber and inscriptions, and the find was reported as the tomb of Gilgamesh, the legendary king of Uruk.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The identification rests on the location and on the epic's statement that Gilgamesh was buried in the bed of the river. The structure's date and function are not established, the inscriptions are not published as naming him, and the site has been inaccessible since.

The archive files it as live debate because the structure exists and the attribution does not follow.

Where it stands

Documented: the structure's existence, as reported.

Not published: the inscriptions in full.

Contested: the identification.

Plausible: the location, from the epic.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the epic and the river agree and the archaeology has not caught up.
