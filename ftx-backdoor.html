FTX's Backdoor

An exchange took customer deposits and lent them to a related trading firm, using a hidden exemption in its accounting. It collapsed in days.

The structure

FTX was a cryptocurrency exchange; Alameda Research was a trading firm founded by the same person, Sam Bankman-Fried. Customer deposits were transferred to Alameda, and FTX's systems included a feature that allowed Alameda to maintain a negative balance without triggering the automatic liquidation that would have applied to any other account.

The collapse

In November 2022 a leaked balance sheet showed Alameda's holdings were largely in illiquid tokens, and a rival exchange's decision to sell its FTX token triggered a run. The company collapsed within days; Bankman-Fried was convicted of fraud and conspiracy in 2023 and sentenced to 25 years.

The bankruptcy examiner and the prosecutors documented the backdoor and the commingling in detail. Customer funds were partly recovered.

What is established

Documented: the commingling, the backdoor and the collapse.

Documented: the conviction and sentence.

Documented: the scale of the shortfall.

Contested: the precise allocation of responsibility among executives.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the fastest large fraud in financial history, and because the evidence was a database.
