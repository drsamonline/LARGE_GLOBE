Chavín Gold Plating

A culture that plated copper with gold using electrochemical methods, or so the claim goes. The objects are real; the method is argued.

The objects

Chavín and related Andean cultures produced objects of gilded copper, with a thin gold surface over a copper core. The plating is documented in museum analyses.

Maps, diagrams and images

The method

Proposals include depletion gilding (removing copper from the surface alloy), foil wrapping and electrochemical replacement. The electrochemical proposal — using acidic solutions and natural batteries — has been suggested for later Andean cultures and is not established for Chavín.

Where it stands

Documented: the gilded objects.

Documented: the thin gold layer.

Contested: the method.

Not established: electrochemical plating.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where 'they had electroplating' is a claim about chemistry rather than magic.
