Entropic Gravity

The proposal that gravity is not a fundamental force but an emergent consequence of entropy. It is elegant, it made a testable prediction, and the test disagreed.

The proposal

Erik Verlinde argued in 2009 that gravity emerges from the thermodynamics of information: the force is an entropic effect, like the tension in a polymer, rather than a fundamental interaction. The proposal built on earlier work by Jacobson and Padmanabhan.

Maps, diagrams and images

The test

A 2016 extension made a specific prediction about the behaviour of gravity in galaxies with low acceleration, offering an alternative to dark matter. Galaxy rotation and lensing data have been used to test it, and the results have not supported the prediction: the theory struggles to reproduce the observed mass discrepancies without dark matter.

The programme continues in modified-gravity research, and the original idea remains a serious conjecture rather than a supported theory.

Where it stands

Documented: the proposal and its extensions.

Documented: the predictions.

Not supported: by galaxy-scale tests.

Not excluded: as a theoretical possibility.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where an elegant idea made a testable prediction and the data went the other way, which is how physics is supposed to work.
