The Carbohydrate–Insulin Model

The claim that obesity is driven by insulin, and that carbohydrate restriction is therefore uniquely effective. The trials find that, matched for protein and calories, the diets perform similarly.

The model

The carbohydrate–insulin model holds that high carbohydrate intake raises insulin, which promotes fat storage and suppresses fat release, so that energy is partitioned into fat and the person becomes hungry and sedentary. On this account, calorie restriction fails because it does not address the hormonal cause.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Low-carbohydrate diets do produce weight loss, and often more in the short term, largely because of water loss and reduced intake. In controlled feeding studies matched for protein and calories, low-carbohydrate and low-fat diets produce similar fat loss. Insulin resistance does not reliably predict who benefits most.

The model's defenders argue that the trials do not test the mechanism adequately, and the argument continues.

Where it stands

Documented: short-term weight loss on low-carbohydrate diets.

Documented: similar fat loss in matched feeding studies.

Contested: the hormonal mechanism.

Contested: individual variation in response.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a mechanism that sounds decisive and meets trials that are unimpressed, and because the practical advice is the same either way.
