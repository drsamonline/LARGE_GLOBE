The Chinese Word for Crisis

The claim that 'crisis' in Chinese means danger plus opportunity. It does not.

The claim

A widely repeated motivational claim holds that the Chinese word for crisis, 危機 (wēijī), is composed of the characters for 'danger' and 'opportunity'. It has been used by politicians and business writers for decades.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

The second character, 機, means a machine, mechanism or crucial point, and 'opportunity' is a stretch of a related sense; the compound is documented in Chinese sources for centuries meaning a crisis or turning point. Linguists have shown that the 'opportunity' reading was popularised in English in the twentieth century.

What is established

Documented: the characters and the compound.

Not the meaning: opportunity.

Popularised: in the 20th century.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a folk etymology that became management advice.
