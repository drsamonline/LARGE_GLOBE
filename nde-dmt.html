Near-Death Experiences as DMT Release

The claim that the brain releases DMT at death, producing the tunnel, the light and the life review. The resemblance is real; the release has never been measured.

The claim

The claim is that N,N-dimethyltryptamine, a psychedelic compound found in some plants and in trace amounts in mammals, is released in large quantities by the dying brain, and that this produces the characteristic features of near-death experiences: the tunnel, the light, the life review, the sense of peace and the encounters.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

DMT has been detected in rodent brain tissue, at very low concentrations, and its function is unknown. A 2019 study reported a rise in DMT in rat brains after cardiac arrest, which is suggestive and not evidence of a human mechanism. No study has measured a DMT surge in dying humans, and the doses required to produce the experience are far above the trace levels found.

Other explanations for NDEs — hypoxia, hypercapnia, temporal lobe activity, end-of-life neurochemistry — are also incomplete.

Where it stands

Documented: NDE phenomenology; trace DMT in mammals.

Reported: a rise after cardiac arrest, in rats.

Not measured: any surge in dying humans.

Contested: the mechanism generally.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most plausible pharmacological explanation offered for NDEs, and it has not been demonstrated.
