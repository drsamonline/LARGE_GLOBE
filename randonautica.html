Randonautica

An app that generates a random coordinate and sends you there, promising 'anomalies'. In 2020 two teenagers following one found a suitcase containing human remains, and the app became famous for the wrong reason.

The app

Randonautica generates coordinates near the user — 'attractors', 'voids', 'anomalies' — using a random number generator seeded in part by the user's intention. Users walk to the point and report what they find. The framing borrows from quantum randomness and the observer effect.

Maps, diagrams and images

What happened

In 2020, two teenagers in Washington state followed a coordinate into a wooded area and found a suitcase containing human remains, which led to a murder investigation and a great deal of coverage about the app's 'accuracy'. The coordinates are random; a random point in a populated area will sometimes contain something.

The statistics are the answer. Millions of walks have been generated; the number of notable finds is what you would expect from chance plus selection — the finds get posted, the empty walks do not.

What is established

Documented: the app, its random generation, and the 2020 case.

Documented: the mechanism is chance plus selection.

Not established: any link between intention and outcome.

Not established: any 'anomaly' beyond ordinary finds.

The lesson: a random destination is a good way to notice your surroundings, and a bad way to test a hypothesis.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the frequency illusion with a map attached, and because it produced one genuinely useful outcome — a solved case — from a mechanism that explains nothing.
