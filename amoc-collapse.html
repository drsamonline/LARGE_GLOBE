AMOC Collapse

The Atlantic overturning circulation may weaken or collapse. The physics is documented; the timing and likelihood are argued.

The system

The Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation carries warm surface water north and cold deep water south, moderating European climate. Climate models project weakening as freshwater from Greenland and rainfall increases.

Maps, diagrams and images

The debate

Observations are short and uncertain, and reconstructions disagree: some proxies suggest a decline, others do not. The IPCC assesses weakening as likely this century and collapse as unlikely before 2100, while some studies argue the risk is underestimated. The consequences would be severe.

The archive files it as live debate.

Where it stands

Documented: the circulation and its role.

Likely: weakening.

Contested: the rate and the collapse risk.

Uncertain: the observational record.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the climate risk where the disagreement is about timing rather than direction.
