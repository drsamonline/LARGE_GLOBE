The Phobos Monolith

A boulder casting a long shadow on the Martian moon was described by a former astronaut as a 'monolith'. It is a boulder, about 85 metres across, on a moon covered in boulders.

The object

In 1998 the Mars Global Surveyor photographed Phobos and returned an image containing a small, bright, roughly rectangular object casting a long shadow across the surface. The image was circulated with the claim that it was a monolith — an artificial structure — and the claim was amplified when Buzz Aldrin referred to 'a monolith' on Phobos in a 2009 interview.

Maps, diagrams and images

What the image shows

The object is about 85 metres across, sitting in a cratered landscape. It casts a shadow consistent with a boulder of that size under a low sun. Phobos is covered in boulders — the same mission imaged many — and a rectangular-looking boulder is not a surprising shape for a fractured ejecta block.

Aldrin's remark was about a boulder and about the oddity of a single large block on a small moon; it was reported as though he had said 'structure'. He has since been clear that he was describing a rock.

What is established

Documented: a large boulder on Phobos, imaged in 1998, casting a shadow.

Documented: the moon is covered in boulders of comparable origin.

Not established: any artificial structure, or any geometric regularity beyond a blocky shape.

Not concealed: the image was publicly released by the mission that took it.

The pattern: the same as the Face on Mars — a real object, a suggestive shape, and a claim that outruns the resolution.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the same error as the Face on Mars in a smaller frame, and because it shows how a careful sentence from a credible person ('a monolith') becomes a claim about aliens once it passes through headlines.
