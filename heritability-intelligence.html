Heritability of Intelligence

Twin and adoption studies put the heritability of adult IQ high. What that number means, and what it does not, is where the argument lives.

The finding

Twin and adoption studies consistently find that the heritability of IQ rises with age: in childhood it is around 0.4–0.5, and in adulthood estimates reach 0.7–0.8. Identical twins reared apart are more similar than fraternal twins reared together, and adopted children resemble their biological parents more as they age.

Maps, diagrams and images

What it means

Heritability is the proportion of variance in a population attributable to genetic variation. It says nothing about the causes of a difference between groups, nothing about whether a trait can be changed, and nothing about an individual. A high heritability does not imply immutability — height is highly heritable and has risen sharply with nutrition.

The disputes are about the interpretation, about the environmental assumptions of twin models, and about the uses to which the numbers have been put historically.

Where it stands

Documented: the twin and adoption findings.

Documented: heritability increasing with age.

Contested: the interpretation and the models' assumptions.

Not implied: immutability, or group differences.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most abused statistic in the social sciences, and because the correct reading is narrower than either side usually admits.
