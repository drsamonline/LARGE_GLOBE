The Betz Sphere

A steel sphere found after a forest fire was said to move on its own, hum, and resist investigation. It is a ball bearing from industrial machinery.

The object

In 1974 a family in Florida found a metal sphere about 20 cm across in the aftermath of a forest fire on their property. They reported that it moved on its own, that it hummed, and that it behaved oddly when placed on a glass table. It was covered widely, including by newspapers and a television programme.

Maps, diagrams and images

The identification

The sphere was examined and identified as a stainless steel ball — a large ball bearing of a kind used in industrial machinery, with a manufacturing seam. Analyses found no mechanism, no power source and no anomaly. The movement reports were consistent with a heavy sphere on an uneven or vibrating surface, and with the expectations of the people watching it.

The sphere was later reported lost or stolen, which is the usual afterlife of an object whose mystery has run out.

What is established

Documented: the object, and its identification as an industrial ball bearing.

Documented: the absence of any mechanism or power source.

Not established: any anomalous behaviour under controlled conditions.

The error: reports of motion treated as measurements.

The honest label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the 1970s version of a viral object, and because the debunking is a single sentence: it is a ball bearing.
