The Replication Crisis

Large numbers of published findings in psychology and medicine failed to replicate. The failure is documented, and so are its causes.

The finding

Starting with a 2011 project that repeated 100 psychology studies and reproduced fewer than half the original effects, large-scale replication efforts in psychology, cancer biology and economics found that many published results were weaker than reported or absent.

Maps, diagrams and images

The causes

Documented contributors include small samples, flexible analysis that produces false positives, selective reporting of results, and publication that favours positive findings. Reforms include pre-registration, registered reports and data sharing, which are being adopted unevenly.
