Will Died in Bel-Air

The theory that Will Smith's character died in the first episode and the series is his afterlife. It explains nothing that needs explaining, and it is a joke.

The theory

The theory holds that Will, the narrator of The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air, was killed in the fight that opens the series, and that the sitcom is his imagined afterlife — with the Banks family as figures in it, and the laughter as a symptom of unreality.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The show establishes that Will survived the fight and was sent to live with his aunt. The theory requires the narration to be unreliable in a way the series never suggests, and it imports a dark reading into a family sitcom. It has no support from the creators.

It belongs to a genre of 'the sitcom is purgatory' theories that can be applied to any show with a premise.

What is established

Documented: the theory.

Documented: the show's actual premise.

Not established: any death.

The method: genre transplant.

The label: fiction.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the clearest example of a theory that could be applied to any work and therefore explains nothing.
