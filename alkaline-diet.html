The Alkaline Diet

The claim that food changes blood pH and that acidity causes disease. The body regulates pH within a narrow range.

The claim

The alkaline diet holds that acidic foods cause disease and that eating alkaline foods changes blood pH, improving health and treating cancer. It is promoted in books and by some celebrities.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Blood pH is tightly regulated by the kidneys and lungs, and food does not meaningfully change it; urine pH does change, which is where the confusion comes from. The diet's advice overlaps with ordinary healthy eating, and its cancer claims are unsupported.

What is established

Documented: the claim.

False: the pH mechanism.

Real: urine pH changes.

Unsupported: the cancer claims.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the advice is harmless and the reason for it is wrong.

Why the premise fails

The claim is that diet can shift the body's pH toward alkaline and that this prevents disease. Blood pH is held within a narrow range by buffers and by the lungs and kidneys; food does not move it, and when it does move the result is a medical emergency.

What diet does affect is urine pH, which is where the measurement in these claims usually comes from. The archive files it as debunked because the premise is testable and fails, while the advice to eat more vegetables is sound for reasons the theory does not supply.
