The Gleiwitz Incident

German agents staged an attack on a radio station and blamed Poland, as the pretext for invasion. Prisoners were killed and left as 'casualties'.

The operation

On the night of 31 August 1939, SS personnel under Alfred Naujocks staged an attack on the radio station at Gleiwitz, then in Germany (now Gliwice, Poland). They broadcast a short anti-German message in Polish, left a body on the steps, and presented the event as a Polish provocation.

Maps, diagrams and images

The record

The operation was part of a wider set of staged incidents along the border, and it was used in Hitler's speech announcing the invasion the next day. At Nuremberg, Naujocks testified to the operation and to the killing of a prisoner — referred to by the code name 'Canned Goods' — to provide a corpse.

The incident is one of the best-documented false flags in history, and it is the standard example used whenever the category is discussed.

What is established

Documented: the operation, the participants and the staging.

Documented: the death of a prisoner to provide a body.

Documented: the Nuremberg testimony.

Documented: the use as a pretext.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the historical false flag that all later claims are measured against, and because it was admitted by its own organiser.
