The Anthropocene

Humans have changed the rock record. Whether that deserves a place in the geological timescale was put to a vote in 2024 and lost — and the vote is more interesting than the answer.

The proposal

Paul Crutzen and Eugene Stoermer proposed the term in 2000, arguing that human activity had become a geological force. The Anthropocene Working Group, convened in 2009, spent fifteen years on the question of whether it should be a formal unit of the geological timescale — an epoch, following the Holocene — and if so, where its base should be.

Maps, diagrams and images

In 2016 the group voted for a mid-twentieth-century start, tied to the Great Acceleration: the sharp rise in population, energy use, fertiliser, plastics and carbon dioxide after 1950. The marker would be plutonium fallout from atmospheric nuclear tests, which appears worldwide in sediments and ice and did not exist before 1945.

The vote

In March 2024 the proposal was rejected by the International Union of Geological Sciences. The reason was not that the evidence is weak. It was procedural and definitional: a geological epoch is defined by a synchronous global boundary in rock, and the Anthropocene's boundary is not synchronous — it arrives at different times in different places, and much of the evidence is in sediments that will not survive to become rock.

The working group's alternative is to treat it as an event rather than an epoch, which is how geology handles things like the Great Oxidation Event: a period of change recorded in the rock, without a formal line.

What is settled and what is not

Documented: humans have altered the physical record — radionuclides, plastics, concrete, technofossils, altered sediment transport, a changed carbon cycle.

Documented: the changes accelerated sharply around 1950.

Rejected: the formal epoch, on stratigraphic rather than evidential grounds.

Still argued: the start date. Proposals include 1610 (a dip in atmospheric CO₂ after the Americas were depopulated), the Industrial Revolution, and 1950.

The honest position: the phenomenon is real and the naming is politics. A word that describes a fact and a word that defines a boundary are not the same word.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the rare case where the contested part is the label rather than the evidence, and where the scientists who proposed it lost the vote and accepted the result. It is what a live debate looks like when the participants follow the rules.
