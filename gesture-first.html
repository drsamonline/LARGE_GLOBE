Gesture or Voice First

Did language begin in the hands or the mouth? The evidence is indirect, and the debate has moved with discoveries about mirror systems, sign languages and ape gestures.

The question

Language leaves no fossil record, so the question of whether gesture or speech came first has to be answered indirectly. The gesture-first hypothesis proposes that manual communication preceded vocal language, and that speech took over later. The voice-first hypothesis proposes that vocal calls were the substrate from the start.

The evidence

Support for gesture-first includes the manual gestures of great apes, the structure of sign languages (which show the full properties of language), the role of gesture in child development, and the overlap between gesture and speech in the brain. Support for voice-first includes the antiquity of vocal control, the anatomy of the vocal tract, and the difficulty of using hands while making tools.

Both sides rely on indirect evidence, and the field is divided.

Where it stands

Documented: ape gesture, sign language structure, gesture-speech coupling.

Documented: vocal anatomy and control in humans and ancestors.

Contested: which came first.

Not established: any direct evidence of the order.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a major scientific question with no direct evidence, which is a useful contrast with the pages where the absence of evidence is the finding.
