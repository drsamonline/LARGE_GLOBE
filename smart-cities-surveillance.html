Smart Cities as Surveillance Grids

The claim that smart-city projects are a cover for total surveillance. The sensors are real; the total grid is not, and the projects have mostly been smaller and duller.

The claim

The claim holds that smart-city programmes — sensor networks, traffic management, connected lighting — are a front for comprehensive surveillance of populations. Some deployments have indeed included cameras and data collection, and some have been controversial.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Investigations of smart-city projects have found modest deployments: air-quality sensors, parking sensors, traffic cameras, and pilot programmes that were often cancelled. Several high-profile projects failed for cost and governance reasons rather than for surveillance.

The honest position is that urban sensor networks raise real privacy questions, and that the 'total grid' claim is not supported by what has been built.

What is established

Documented: sensor deployments and their limits.

Documented: privacy concerns and controversies.

Documented: failed and cancelled projects.

Not established: a comprehensive surveillance grid.

The label: debunked, with the concerns noted.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the worry is legitimate and the specific claim overstates what exists.
