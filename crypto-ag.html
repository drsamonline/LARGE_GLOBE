Crypto AG

A Swiss company sold encryption machines to governments around the world for decades. The CIA and the German BND owned it, and the machines were compromised by design.

The operation

Crypto AG was a Swiss manufacturer of cipher machines, sold to governments, militaries and businesses worldwide from the 1950s. From 1970 the CIA and the West German BND secretly owned the company through a Liechtenstein foundation, and the machines were designed so that the owners could decrypt the traffic.

Maps, diagrams and images

The operation, code-named Rubicon, gave Western intelligence access to the communications of dozens of countries for decades — including, at times, states that were not adversaries.

The exposure

The story was published in 2020 by the Washington Post, ZDF and Swiss television, based on declassified documents and interviews. The company was sold off in the 1990s, and the Swiss government had known of the arrangement for years without acting.

What is established

Documented: the ownership, the compromised design and the duration.

Documented: the 2020 exposure, with documents.

Documented: the customers, in outline.

Not fully documented: which traffic was read and when.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the strongest possible counter-example to 'they could never keep it secret' — they did, for fifty years.
