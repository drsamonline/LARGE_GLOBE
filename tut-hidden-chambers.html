Hidden Chambers in Tut's Tomb

A claim that Nefertiti's tomb lies behind the walls of Tutankhamun's burial chamber, based on radar scans. The scans were repeated, and the chambers were not there.

The claim

In 2015 Nicholas Reeves argued that high-resolution images of Tutankhamun's tomb showed traces of two sealed doorways, and that one of them could lead to the burial of Nefertiti. The claim was widely reported and raised hopes of a major discovery.

Maps, diagrams and images

The tests

Radar surveys in 2015 and 2016 produced conflicting results; a further survey in 2017–2018 by a different team found no evidence of chambers or doorways behind the walls. Ground-penetrating radar and thermographic surveys found nothing, and the Egyptian authorities concluded that there were no hidden chambers.

The episode is a clean case of a hypothesis being tested and rejected, and it is worth noting that the original claim was made by an Egyptologist, argued from evidence, and published.

What is established

Documented: the hypothesis and its basis in the imagery.

Documented: the radar and thermographic surveys.

Not found: any hidden chamber.

Rejected: by the subsequent surveys.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it shows the archive's method working in a live discipline: a good hypothesis, a decisive test, and a negative result.
