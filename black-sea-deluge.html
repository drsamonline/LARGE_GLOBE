The Black Sea Deluge

A hypothesis that the Black Sea flooded catastrophically when the Mediterranean broke through. The flood is argued; its scale and date are not settled.

The hypothesis

In 1997 two marine geologists proposed that around 7,600 years ago the Mediterranean broke through the Bosphorus and flooded the Black Sea basin, raising its level rapidly and displacing populations. The proposal was linked to flood myths and to the spread of farming.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Sediment cores, shell chemistry and shoreline data have been used both for and against. Critics argue the Black Sea was already brackish and the rise was gradual; supporters point to drowned shorelines and abrupt faunal changes. A 2000s review found the evidence inconclusive.

The archive files it as live debate: the water rose, and how fast is the question.

Where it stands

Documented: the rise of the Black Sea.

Contested: its speed and scale.

Contested: the link to flood myths.

Inconclusive: the core evidence.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real geological event that has been recruited for a very old story.
