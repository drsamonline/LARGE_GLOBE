The Walking Moai

How were the Easter Island statues moved? A 2012 experiment showed they can be 'walked' upright with ropes, which matches the island's oral tradition.

The question

The moai of Easter Island — hundreds of statues, some weighing tens of tonnes — were moved from the quarry at Rano Raraku to platforms around the island. How they were transported has been argued for decades, with proposals including rollers, sledges and levers.

The experiment

In 2012 Terry Hunt and Carl Lipo tested the 'walking' hypothesis: with a replica statue and three ropes, a team rocked the figure upright and moved it forward by alternating pulls, covering tens of metres in under an hour. The method matches the island's oral tradition that the statues walked, and it explains the shape of the bases and the wear patterns.

The experiment does not prove that this is what happened, but it demonstrates that it could have, without rollers or timber — which matters on a deforested island.

What is established

Documented: the statues, their transport and their platforms.

Demonstrated: that upright rocking works at replica scale.

Documented: the oral tradition of walking statues.

Contested: whether it was the method used.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where an experiment and a tradition agreed, and the answer was more interesting than either the mystery or the myth.
