Timewave Zero

A curve derived from the I Ching, claimed to map the novelty of the universe and to end in 2012. The date passed; the curve was fitted, not derived.

The claim

Terence McKenna developed 'timewave zero' from 1975: a graph of novelty — the rate of change and complexity in the universe — derived, he said, from the sequence of hexagrams in the I Ching. The curve was fractal, self-similar at every scale, and was claimed to describe the past and predict the future.

Its endpoint, the 'zero point' of infinite novelty, was set at 21 December 2012, matching the end of the Maya long count.

The problems

The derivation is arbitrary. The King Wen sequence of hexagrams is a historical ordering with no known mathematical necessity, and the transformation from that sequence to a curve involves choices that are not constrained by anything. When the curve failed to match history, the parameters were adjusted.

The date was also adjustable: earlier versions of the theory put the zero point in 2011, and the choice of 2012 aligned it with a separate cultural bandwagon. The date passed without an event, which is the kind of test the claim could not survive.

What is established

Documented: the theory, its construction, and its 2012 endpoint.

Documented: the 2012 date passed without incident.

Not derived: the curve from any measurement. It is a construction with free parameters.

Not predictive: no successful prediction that was made in advance and survived.

Why the label is debunked: it made a dated prediction, the date passed, and the claim was not revised into a testable form.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the archive's clearest example of a claim with a hard date attached — and hard dates are the best gift a claim can give a reader, because they can be checked.
