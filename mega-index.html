The 200

Two hundred theories, legends, paradoxes and conspiracies from one collection, each labelled with what the evidence says — and linked to the dossier that already covers it.

How to read this index

This is an index, not two hundred dossiers. Each row gives the claim in one line and the label the archive would give it. Where a full dossier already exists, the title is a link; where it does not, the label tells you what the state of the evidence is and the sources below cover the general patterns.

The labels: Documented — the thing exists or happened, and is on the record. Live debate — real evidence, real specialists, no settled answer. Fringe — no positive evidence, often unfalsifiable in practice. Debunked — tested and failed, or the product of a known hoax. Fiction — invented story, fan theory or internet lore, labelled as such rather than as a claim about the world.

What this collection gets wrong

A few of the 200 are stated in a form the evidence no longer supports, and the index marks them rather than repeating the framing:

  • Tabby's Star is no longer a Dyson-sphere candidate: the dimming is best explained by dust (Boyajian et al., 2018).
  • The Face on Mars dissolved when Mars Global Surveyor imaged the mesa at higher resolution in 1998 and 2001.
  • The Piri Reis map does not show an ice-free Antarctica; that reading came from Hapgood in 1966, not from the map.
  • The Baghdad Battery and Dendera "lights" are both well-explained without electricity — as a scroll container and as lotus-and-snake cosmology.
  • Crystal skulls carry modern tool marks; the British Museum and others have documented manufacture in the 19th century.
  • Olmec heads as African is a claim with a racist history and no anatomical support.
  • "Phantom cosmonaut" tapes are real recordings of disputed origin, not proof of secret deaths.
