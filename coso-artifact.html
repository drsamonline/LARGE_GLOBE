The Coso Artifact

A 'geode' from California was found to contain a spark plug. It was said to be half a million years old. It is a 1920s Champion spark plug in a concretion.

The find

In 1961 a group collecting minerals near Olancha, California, found a nodule that appeared to be a geode. When it was cut open, it contained what looked like a spark plug, along with a metal washer and a nail-like object. The story, published in a treasure-hunting magazine, dated the nodule to around half a million years.

Maps, diagrams and images

The identification

Spark plug collectors identified the object as a Champion spark plug of a type made from the 1920s onward, with a design that matches a specific model. The 'geode' is a concretion — a mass of iron-rich sediment that had formed around the object, which is exactly how such nodules form around debris in wet ground.

The dating came from the assumption that the nodule was a geode formed in ancient rock, not from any measurement of the object. Once the object is identified, the age follows from the object, not the nodule.

What is established

Documented: the object, its find and its identification as a 1920s spark plug.

Documented: the concretion mechanism that encased it.

Not established: any ancient origin.

The error: dating the container and attributing the date to the contents.

The honest label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the cleanest example of the container-versus-contents error, which appears in several pages here, and because the identification took a specialist collector ten minutes.
