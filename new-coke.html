New Coke as a Trojan Horse

Coca-Cola replaced its recipe in 1985, the public revolted, and the original returned in 79 days. The claim is that the whole thing was a planned manoeuvre.

The events

In April 1985 Coca-Cola replaced its formula with a sweeter version, 'New Coke', after taste tests showed it beat both Pepsi and the original. The reaction was immediate and hostile; the company restored the original as 'Coca-Cola Classic' in July, seventy-nine days later.

The claim

The conspiracy reading is that the whole episode was planned to reinvigorate the brand and to convert customers from Pepsi back to Coke, and that the backlash was anticipated. It is an attractive story: the company got a huge amount of free publicity and emerged with a stronger position.

The evidence is against it. Internal documents and interviews with executives describe genuine alarm, emergency meetings and a scramble to reverse the decision; the company's stock and sales fell during the period. A planned failure that costs you market share and requires an apology is a strange plan.

Where it stands

Documented: the reformulation, the backlash, the reversal.

Documented: the internal reaction, from documents and interviews.

Not established: any plan to fail.

Contested: how much of the eventual benefit was luck.

The honest label: fringe — a good story with the record against it.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best example of a conspiracy theory that would make the world more interesting if it were true, and because the documents are the answer.
