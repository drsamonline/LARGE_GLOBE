Corrections

Every page here is one person's reading of primary sources, checked against the record and open to being wrong. This page says what counts as an error, how to report one, and what happens to the page afterwards.

What countsFive kinds of error, in order of seriousness

  1. A misattributed claim. Someone is credited with an argument they did not make, or a quotation is attributed to the wrong author or year. This is the error the archive cares most about, because attribution is its entire method.
  2. A wrong verdict. A claim labelled debunked that has since been supported, or one labelled documented whose evidence does not survive. Labels are re-argued, not quietly softened.
  3. A dead or wrong source. A link that no longer resolves, or one that never said what the page says it said.
  4. A factual error. A date, a number, a name, a location.
  5. A licence or credit error. An image credited to the wrong author, or reproduced under a licence it does not carry.

How to reportOne link, no account

The fastest route is the dead-source issue form — it asks for the page, the exact link or sentence, and what is wrong. Email works too: sohilmomin2000@gmail.com. You do not need to be right about the fix; being right about the problem is enough.

Useful, in this order: the page URL, the sentence or link, the source that contradicts it, and your name if you want to be credited. Anonymous corrections are accepted and acted on.

What happensAnnotate first, delete second

A corrected page is re-dated and the change is described in the commit. Where a source has rotted rather than been wrong, the link is replaced with an archived copy and the page says so. Where a claim has been overtaken by evidence, the verdict is changed and the page explains what moved it — the original wording is not quietly removed, because a reference work that erases its own history is not a reference work.

Nothing is deleted for being embarrassing. A page that was wrong and is now right is more useful than a page that was never written.

LimitsWhat this archive is not

It is not peer reviewed, and every page says so. It is not a primary source: the sources are linked, and the linked source always outranks the page that cites it. It has no editorial board, no sponsor with a veto over a label, and no corrections queue that depends on anyone's approval but the reader's patience.

Where a page states a probability, a date or a figure, the number is either measured on the page or attributed to the source that measured it. Where it is neither, that is a bug — report it.
