Voynich Alien Botany

The Voynich manuscript's plants do not match any known species, which has been read as evidence of an alien or otherworldly origin. The plants are drawings in a medieval herbal tradition.

The manuscript

The Voynich manuscript is an illustrated codex, carbon-dated to the early fifteenth century, written in an unknown script. Its botanical section shows plants that do not match known species exactly, along with astronomical diagrams and bathing scenes.

Maps, diagrams and images

The claim and the explanation

The claim that the plants are alien — or that the manuscript records another world — rests on the failure to identify them. Medieval herbals routinely contain stylised, composite and copied-from-copies illustrations, and identification is often impossible for genuine plants, let alone for drawings made at several removes from the original.

The manuscript is a real and unsolved problem — the script is undeciphered — but the plants are not evidence of extraterrestrial origin, and the 'alien' reading adds nothing that can be tested.

What is established

Documented: the manuscript, its date and its sections.

Documented: the herbal tradition it belongs to.

Not established: any alien origin.

Undeciphered: the script.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real mystery with a real explanation for the part that looks strangest, and because the unexplained part is the text.
