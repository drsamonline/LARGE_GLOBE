Flat Earth & the Firmament

A disc with the North Pole at the centre, a wall of ice at the rim, and a dome overhead. The model is simple. What is interesting is how it survives.

Flat Earth is the oldest cosmology in the archive and the only one with a continuously operating institution. It also has the most clearly defined internal structure, which makes it the best place to learn how these systems work: every anomaly has a patch, and the patches are invented on demand.

Maps, diagrams and images

What the modern model actually claims

There is no single flat-Earth model. Britannica’s survey identifies the common form — a circular disc, North Pole at the centre, a wall of ice at the edges — and then two competing mechanical variants:

  • The “US model” — the Sun and Moon are small objects, roughly 50 km across, circling about 5,500 km above the disc, with the stars fixed to a rotating dome above them. Day and night come from the Sun acting like a spotlight.
  • The “UK model” — no gravity at all; the disc accelerates upward at 9.8 m/s², and what we call falling is the floor rising to meet us.

Physics World’s 2020 survey of the movement notes that many adherents also place the whole disc and its atmosphere inside a hemispherical “snow globe” from which nothing can fall off the edge — which resolves the obvious objection that we would see the rim.

Their evidence, in their own words

The Flat Earth Society’s own FAQ lists the standard four:

“The horizon always rises to meet eye level — which is impossible on a ball earth. The surfaces of bodies of water has been shown to be level. If the Earth was a Globe, this would not be the case. There is no visible curvature to the horizon even from airplanes. We don’t even have a full shot of the Earth rotating from space!” The Flat Earth Society, FAQ

Each is answerable in a sentence, and the answers have been published for two centuries. The horizon rises to eye level because the eye is only a few metres above a sphere 12,742 km across; at 10 km altitude the true horizon drop is about 3.2°, small but measurable. Water is level locally — a plumb line is perpendicular to the surface at every point, which on a sphere means every point has its own “level”. And full-disc rotating imagery exists from geostationary weather satellites; the 2015 DSCOVR “EPIC” camera returns a full sunlit disc from a million miles out.

Where the movement came from

The modern revival is usually dated to Samuel Birley Rowbotham, writing as “Parallax”, and his 1849 pamphlet expanded into Zetetic Astronomy. His signature demonstration was the Bedford Level experiment: place markers at equal height at either end of a six-mile straight canal, view them through a telescope, and if both appear level, the water is flat.

It worked — and it was quoted as proof for over thirty years. The flaw was the atmosphere: light bends over a long, warm, still water surface, lifting the distant marker back into view. Alfred Russel Wallace eventually repeated the experiment with a calibrated sight and won a libel case over the resulting dispute. The Conversation’s history of the episode is the clearest short account.

The firmament

The dome is the theological layer. In the King James Bible, Genesis 1:6–8 has God make a rāqîa‘ to divide the waters, and the Authorised Version translates it “firmament” — a word that in English implies something solid. Hebrew scholarship is close to unanimous that rāqîa‘ means an expanse or spreading-out, not a crystal vault, and the same root is used for the expanse of the sky.

This produces an unusual split. Young-Earth creationist organisations — hardly friendly witnesses to mainstream science — have published detailed refutations of flat Earth, arguing that the Hebrew does not support a solid dome and that verses about the “circle of the earth” (khûg, meaning roundness) cannot be reduced to a two-dimensional disc. The Adventist Biblical Research Institute reaches the same conclusion independently.

What the evidence says

Gravity is the fatal problem. A disc has a centre of mass at its middle, so gravity would pull everything toward that point, not straight down. James Davis of Columbia’s Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory puts it simply: on a disc, water would flow to the centre and trees would grow at an angle. There is no way to derive the observed 9.8 m/s² everywhere from a thin disc.

The geometry is checkable with a phone. Eratosthenes’ shadow measurement, the differing altitude of Polaris with latitude, the southern sky’s rotation about a different pole, the 24-hour sun of the Antarctic summer, and the existence of time zones all follow from a sphere and contradict a spotlight sun over a disc.

Antarctica is a continent, not a rim. Reuters and USA Today have both fact-checked the ice-wall claim with satellite imagery, circumnavigation records and the simple fact that people live and work there year-round. The most-shared “flat Earth map” with concentric ice rings has been traced to an art-forum illustration.

Why it persists anyway

Physics World’s report frames it as a trust problem rather than a geometry problem: the model’s appeal is that it makes the reader the final authority and the institution the liar. Every failed prediction can be reassigned to “the conspiracy” — which makes the belief unfalsifiable, and therefore not a scientific claim at all.

That structure is worth carrying into the rest of the archive. It is the same move that keeps the inner sun lit and the extra continents hidden.
