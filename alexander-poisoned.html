Alexander Poisoned

Did Alexander the Great die of disease or poison? The sources disagree and the body is lost.

The death

Alexander died in Babylon in 323 BC after an illness of about twelve days. The accounts describe fever, pain and progressive weakness, and they were written centuries later.

Maps, diagrams and images

The explanations

Proposals include malaria, typhoid, alcohol-related illness, Guillain–Barré syndrome and poisoning, with poisoning favoured in some later traditions and by some modern writers. The body has never been identified, and no source is contemporary.

Where it stands

Documented: the illness and its duration.

Documented: the later sources.

Contested: the cause.

Not available: the remains.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most famous unsolved death in antiquity, and the sources are the problem.
