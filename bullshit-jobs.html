Bullshit Jobs

David Graeber's claim that a large share of jobs are pointless and their holders know it. The essay struck a nerve; the survey evidence is thinner than the argument.

The claim

Graeber's 2013 essay, later a book, argued that a large proportion of modern employment is 'bullshit' — jobs the holder believes are pointless — and that this is a structural feature of contemporary capitalism rather than a personal failing. He offered a taxonomy: flunkies, goons, duct tapers, box tickers, taskmasters.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Support comes from testimony and from surveys in which a substantial minority report their jobs as useless. The figures vary widely with the question asked, and critics note that self-report measures satisfaction and meaning rather than economic necessity. Graeber's estimate of the share was not derived from a representative sample.

The archive files it as live debate because the phenomenon is widely reported and the magnitude is not established.

Where it stands

Documented: the essay, the book and the testimony.

Documented: surveys reporting pointless work.

Contested: the proportion.

Contested: the structural explanation.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a claim that many people recognise and no one has measured well.
