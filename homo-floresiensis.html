Homo floresiensis

A metre-tall hominin found on Flores, dated to as recently as 50,000 years ago. The discovery was disputed for a decade and is now accepted.

The find

In 2003 excavations in Liang Bua cave on Flores, Indonesia, produced the remains of a hominin about a metre tall with a small brain, dated to between roughly 100,000 and 50,000 years ago. The species was named Homo floresiensis and nicknamed the hobbit.

Maps, diagrams and images

The dispute

Some researchers argued the remains were modern humans with microcephaly or another pathology. The counter-evidence accumulated: multiple individuals, wrist bones with primitive morphology, and stone tools associated with the site. The species is now widely accepted, though its evolutionary relationships — possibly descended from an early Homo erectus population — are still argued.

The island setting explains the small body size: insular dwarfism is documented in other species.

What is established

Documented: the remains, their dates and their anatomy.

Documented: the stone tools and the cave context.

Accepted: that it is a distinct species.

Contested: its ancestry.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real discovery that was resisted and then accepted, which is how the process is supposed to work.
