The Dorchester Pot

A brass vessel was found in rock in 1852 and dated to the deep past. It is a nineteenth-century ornament, and the 'rock' is a conglomerate that formed around it.

The find

In 1852, workers in Dorchester, Massachusetts, reported finding a bell-shaped metal vessel in rock that was said to be ancient — variously described as Precambrian or as part of a great deposit. The object was reported in a local scientific journal, and has been cited ever since as an out-of-place artefact.

Maps, diagrams and images

The identification

The vessel resembles a nineteenth-century decorative object — a finial or a bedpost fitting, in a style in production at the time — and the rock is a conglomerate, a sedimentary rock made of cemented fragments, which can form around an object relatively quickly. The original report's geological description has been questioned since the nineteenth century.

The object is in a private collection and has not been re-examined with modern methods, which is a common feature of this genre.

What is established

Documented: the find, the report and the object's description.

Documented: the resemblance to a nineteenth-century fitting.

Documented: conglomerates form around objects.

Not established: any ancient origin.

The honest label: debunked, on style and geology rather than on a modern measurement.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is one of the oldest entries in the genre, and because the answer was available in 1852 to anyone who looked at the object's style.
