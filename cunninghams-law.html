Cunningham's Law

The fastest way to get the right answer online is to post the wrong one. It is a joke about correction culture, and it is accurate often enough to be useful.

The law

Cunningham's law is usually stated as: the best way to get the right answer on the internet is not to ask a question, it is to post the wrong answer. It is attributed to Ward Cunningham, the inventor of the wiki, though the attribution is informal and the exact origin is not documented.

Maps, diagrams and images

Why it works

The mechanism is a combination of things that are well documented in online behaviour: people are more motivated to correct an error than to answer a question, corrections are socially rewarded in technical communities, and a confident wrong statement is more visible than a tentative question.

The cost is that the correction may be wrong too, and that the law rewards provocation. It is a heuristic about incentives, not a method for finding truth.

What is established

Documented: the formulation and its informal attribution.

Documented: the behaviour it describes.

Not documented: a specific origin date or statement.

The cost: corrections can be wrong; provocation is rewarded.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it explains a great deal of the archive's subject matter: a confident wrong claim travels further than a careful question.
