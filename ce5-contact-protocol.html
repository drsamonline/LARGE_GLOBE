The CE5 Contact Protocol

Paid events where groups meditate to summon UFOs. Participants report lights in the sky; the lights are usually identifiable, and the protocol has never produced an object anyone could examine.

The protocol

CE5 — close encounter of the fifth kind — is a term popularised by Steven Greer, an American physician and UFO campaigner, for human-initiated contact. Participants gather at night, meditate, and attempt to signal craft with lights or thought. Events are commercial, and some are expensive.

Maps, diagrams and images

What happens

Participants report lights appearing, moving, and responding to signals. The reports are consistent, and the identification rate is not. Analyses of CE5 footage have repeatedly matched the lights to aircraft, satellites, lanterns, flares and, in several cases, to drones flown by other participants or by the organisers.

The pattern is the same as the Starlink panics: a large dark sky, a motivated observer, and a low bar for what counts as a response. The protocol has never produced an object that could be examined, tracked by radar, or photographed at resolution.

What is established

Documented: the events, the protocol and the reports.

Documented: many of the reported lights have mundane identifications.

Not established: any contact, or any craft.

Not established: that the lights respond to signals.

The honest label: debunked as a contact method, while the experience of the participants is real and is a night in a dark place looking up.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the commercial form of the archive's oldest subject, and because the debunking is not about the sincerity of the participants but about the identification of the lights.
