Dighton Rock

A boulder covered in carvings, attributed to Phoenicians, Norse, Portuguese and Indigenous peoples.

The object

Dighton Rock in Massachusetts bears a dense panel of petroglyphs, recorded since the seventeenth century. It has been attributed to Phoenicians, Norse, Portuguese, Chinese and to Indigenous peoples of the region.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

The markings are documented by photographs and drawings, which disagree with each other because of weathering and early recording methods. The current scholarly view favours Indigenous origin, and no inscription has been accepted as a European script.

Where it stands

Documented: the markings.

Documented: the varying records.

Favoured: Indigenous origin.

Not accepted: any European script.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real inscription whose meaning depends on who is reading it.
