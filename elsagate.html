Elsagate

Children's videos on YouTube were infiltrated by content with inappropriate themes, surfaced by recommendation. The scandal was real, the platform response was documented, and the underlying problem has not gone away.

The scandal

In 2017, researchers and journalists documented a category of videos on YouTube and YouTube Kids that used characters from children's media in content involving violence, medical procedures, and sexualised situations. The videos were not marginal: recommendation systems surfaced them alongside legitimate children's content.

Maps, diagrams and images

The name came from a Reddit thread. The coverage led to advertiser withdrawals, a platform response including restricted mode changes, age restrictions and removed channels, and a broader debate about automated moderation at scale.

What it showed

The mechanism was the recommendation engine optimising for watch time, with children as a captive audience and a content farm ecosystem producing whatever held attention. The platform's moderation was reactive, and the categories of content were not obviously illegal, which is why they survived automated filters.

What is established

Documented: the content, its scale, and the recommendation pathway.

Documented: the platform response and the advertiser withdrawals.

Documented: the difficulty of moderating at scale.

Not established: that the problem was solved; later reporting has found variants.

The lesson: an engagement-optimised feed will find the edge of what is permitted, and children are the least able to report it.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the clearest documented case of a recommendation system doing exactly what it was built to do, with an outcome nobody designing it would have chosen.
