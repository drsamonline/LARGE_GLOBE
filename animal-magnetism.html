How Animals Sense Magnetism

Birds, turtles and bacteria navigate by the Earth's field. The mechanism is still argued.

The behaviour

Many animals respond to magnetic fields: migratory birds orient by them, sea turtles use them to navigate, and magnetotactic bacteria align with them. The behaviour is documented in experiments that manipulate the field.

Maps, diagrams and images

The mechanism

Two mechanisms are candidates: magnetite crystals that act as tiny compasses, and a light-dependent chemical process involving the cryptochrome protein, which would make the sense depend on the visual system. Evidence supports both in different species, and neither is fully established in birds.

The archive files it as live debate because the phenomenon is documented and the sensor is not.

Where it stands

Documented: magnetic orientation in many species.

Documented: magnetite in some tissues.

Documented: cryptochrome's light dependence.

Contested: the mechanism in birds.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a sense that exists and cannot yet be fully explained.
