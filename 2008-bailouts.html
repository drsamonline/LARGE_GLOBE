The 2008 Bailouts

Governments bought stakes in banks and insurers. The scale and the recovery are documented; the verdict is argued.

The interventions

In 2008 the US Treasury's Troubled Asset Relief Program and central-bank facilities injected capital into banks, insurers and carmakers; European governments took stakes in their banks. The amounts, recipients and repayments are documented in official accounts.

Maps, diagrams and images

The verdict

Most TARP funds were repaid with interest, and the programme is documented as having cost less than initially feared; the wider economic costs of the recession and the fairness of rescuing shareholders and executives are argued. Criminal referrals produced few convictions.

What is established

Documented: the amounts and repayments.

Documented: the recipients.

Contested: the fairness and the necessity.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the numbers are public and the story told about them often is not.
