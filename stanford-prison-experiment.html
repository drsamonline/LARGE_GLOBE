The Stanford Prison Experiment

The most famous psychology experiment ever run, and the one with the most documented problems.

The study

In 1971 Philip Zimbardo's team assigned students to guard and prisoner roles in a mock prison, and the experiment was stopped after six days. It became the standard evidence that situations make people cruel.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

Archived records and participant accounts published from 2018 show that guards were coached, that the 'prisoner breakdowns' were partly performed, and that the study had no control group. Zimbardo has defended the work; the conclusions are now widely treated as unsupported.

Where it stands

Documented: the study and its records.

Documented: the coaching of guards.

Not supported: the situational conclusions.

Contested: by Zimbardo.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the textbook case of a famous finding that the record does not support.
