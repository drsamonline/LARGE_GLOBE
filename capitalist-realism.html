Capitalist Realism

Mark Fisher's argument that capitalism has become the horizon of thought, so that alternatives are unimaginable. It is a diagnosis, not a measurement.

The argument

Mark Fisher's Capitalist Realism (2009) argues that capitalism has stopped appearing as one system among others and has become the background condition of thought — so that even its critics imagine change only within it. His line that it is easier to imagine the end of the world than the end of capitalism is the book's summary.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The thesis is a cultural diagnosis, and it is not testable in the way a social-science claim is. Critics note that alternatives are discussed constantly, that the claim risks being unfalsifiable, and that the book's examples are selected.

The archive files it as live debate because it is a serious argument with wide influence and no measurable test.

Where it stands

Documented: the book and its influence.

Contested: the thesis.

Not measurable: as stated.

The value: a description of a mood, precisely put.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is an argument about what people can imagine, and this archive is a record of what they did.
