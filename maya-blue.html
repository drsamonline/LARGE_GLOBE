Maya Blue

A pigment that survived a thousand years of tropical rain, and whose chemistry was only explained in the twentieth century.

The pigment

Maya blue is a vivid pigment used in murals and ceramics from around the eighth century. It resists weathering, acids and biodegradation — unusual for an organic pigment in a tropical climate.

Maps, diagrams and images

The chemistry

Research in the twentieth century established that the colour comes from indigo molecules trapped inside the channels of palygorskite, a clay mineral. The encapsulation protects the dye, which explains the durability, and modern reproductions have confirmed it.

The archive files it as documented because the pigment and its structure are both established.

What is established

Documented: the indigo-clay structure.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where ancient craft anticipated a nanomaterial, and the explanation is chemistry.
