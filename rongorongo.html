Rongorongo

A script from Easter Island, carved on wooden tablets, and undeciphered. Only about two dozen objects survive.

The objects

Rongorongo is a system of glyphs carved on wooden tablets and other objects from Easter Island, first reported in the 1860s. About two dozen objects survive, in museums around the world, and the signs — several hundred distinct forms — are catalogued.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problem

No one can read it. The corpus is very small, the objects are scattered and some are fragmentary, and the tradition of reading was lost in the nineteenth century, when the island's population collapsed and the knowledge died with the carriers. Attempts at decipherment have proposed a script of logographs, a mnemonic system, or a partly syllabic script; none is accepted.

There is also a debate about whether the tablets are readable text at all, or a record-keeping or chanting aid.

What is established

Documented: the objects, the signs and their history.

Documented: the loss of the reading tradition.

Not deciphered: the script.

Contested: whether it is writing in the full sense.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real script that was lost with its readers, which is a more sober story than any of the invented ones.
