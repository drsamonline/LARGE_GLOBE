Ocean Worlds Are the Norm

Europa, Enceladus, Titan, Ganymede, Ceres: subsurface oceans may be common. Whether they are habitable is another question.

The evidence

Magnetic and gravity measurements indicate liquid water beneath the ice of Europa and Ganymede; Enceladus ejects plumes containing salts and organic molecules; Titan has a subsurface ocean beneath its hydrocarbon surface. Several dwarf planets may as well.

Maps, diagrams and images

The question

Habitability requires energy, chemistry and time. The plumes of Enceladus offer samples, and Europa Clipper will arrive in 2030. Nothing yet indicates life, and the environments are harsh: no sunlight, high radiation at Europa's surface, and limited chemical gradients in some cases.

The archive files it as live debate.

Where it stands

Documented: subsurface oceans on several moons.

Documented: Enceladus plume chemistry.

Unknown: habitability.

No evidence: of life.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because the most likely place for life in the solar system is now under ice rather than on a surface.
