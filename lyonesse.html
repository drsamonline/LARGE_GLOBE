Lyonesse

A sunken kingdom off Cornwall, drowned in a single night, from which Tristan came. It is literature, and the sea floor has been surveyed.

The legend

Lyonesse appears in Arthurian romance from the thirteenth century: a kingdom off the coast of Cornwall, ruled by a king whose daughter is Tristan's mother, drowned in a single night along with its towns. The story is closely related to the Breton legend of Ys and to the wider European motif of the sunken city.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

There is no geological candidate: the sea between Cornwall and the Isles of Scilly is deep and has been mapped, and the only drowned land in the region is the Scilly archipelago's prehistoric lowland, which was flooded gradually over millennia rather than in a night.

The motif is literary and is shared across cultures, which is what makes it interesting rather than evidential.

What is established

Documented: the legend, its sources and its relatives.

Not found: any sunken kingdom.

Documented: the gradual flooding of the Scilly lowland.

Not plausible: a single-night drowning at that scale.

The label: fiction.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best-known sunken-land story in English, and because it is honestly a story.
