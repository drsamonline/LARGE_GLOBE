Lazar and Element 115

A man who said he worked on alien craft at Area 51 claimed their fuel was element 115, unknown at the time. Element 115 was synthesised in 2003, and it decays in milliseconds.

The claim

In 1989 Robert Lazar said he had worked at a facility near Area 51 reverse-engineering alien craft, and that their propulsion used element 115 — then an undiscovered element — as a stable fuel. He described the craft's operation and the programme.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

Element 115, moscovium, was first synthesised in 2003 by a Russian–American team. It is highly radioactive, with isotopes that decay in milliseconds, and it cannot be accumulated in the quantities a fuel would require. The 'island of stability' predicted for heavier elements does not extend to moscovium in a usable way.

Lazar's employment and education claims have been checked and not supported: no record at the institutes he named, and no degree at the institutions he cited. He has been convicted of a prostitution-related offence and of illegal chemical disposal, both unrelated to the UFO claim but relevant to credibility.

Where it stands

Documented: the claim, and the subsequent synthesis of element 115.

Documented: moscovium's instability.

Not supported: Lazar's employment and education claims.

Not established: any craft or programme.

The honest label: fringe — a story with one apparent prediction that the chemistry refutes.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most influential piece of Area 51 lore, and because the one checkable prediction was checked.
