Little Albert

A baby conditioned to fear a white rat. The study is famous, the child was never identified, and the ethics were appalling.

The study

In 1920 John Watson and Rosalie Rayner conditioned an infant known as Little Albert to fear a white rat by pairing it with a loud noise. The result was presented as evidence that fear can be learned.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The child's identity was never established, the conditioning was not followed up, and the ethics would not be permitted today. Attempts to identify Albert have produced candidates but no confirmation, and the study's generalisation is limited.

Where it stands

Documented: the study.

Not identified: the child.

Not followed up: the effect.

Unethical: by modern standards.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the founding study of behaviourism's most famous claim, and it has no subject.
