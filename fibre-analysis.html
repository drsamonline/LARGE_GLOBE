Fibre Analysis

Fibres transfer between people and places. The transfer is real; the identification is weaker than it sounds.

The method

Fibres recovered from a scene are compared with textiles associated with a suspect, by colour, type and sometimes dye composition. The method is documented and has contributed to convictions.

Maps, diagrams and images

The limits

Fibre comparison generally identifies a class, not an individual, and common fibres are found everywhere. The strength of the evidence depends on rarity and on the transfer circumstances, which are documented as the crux of the argument.

Where it stands

Documented: transfer and comparison.

Documented: class-level identification.

Contested: the strength in individual cases.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because 'consistent with' and 'came from' are different claims, and the difference is the whole case.
