The XYY Supermale

An extra Y chromosome was said to produce aggressive criminals. The claim collapsed when the sampling was examined.

The claim

In the 1960s studies of men in institutions reported an excess of XYY karyotypes and suggested the extra chromosome produced aggression and criminality. The idea entered textbooks and courtrooms.

Maps, diagrams and images

The correction

The excess reflected ascertainment: XYY men were more likely to be identified in institutions. Population screening found XYY men without criminal records, and follow-up studies found no consistent behavioural syndrome. The claim is documented as a case of sampling bias.

What is established

Documented: the karyotype.

Documented: the early studies.

Explained: by ascertainment bias.

Not supported: the behavioural claim.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a genetic claim that was decided by who got tested.
