The Viral Trigger for Schizophrenia

Infections in pregnancy are associated with schizophrenia in the child, and the immune system is implicated. The effect is real and small.

The evidence

Epidemiological studies have found associations between prenatal exposure to influenza and other infections and later schizophrenia, along with season-of-birth effects and associations with autoimmune disease and maternal immune activation. The genetic evidence includes immune-related loci, notably the complement component C4.

Maps, diagrams and images

The limits

The effects are small, the studies are heterogeneous, and confounding is difficult to exclude. The mechanisms proposed — maternal immune activation affecting fetal brain development — are supported by animal models, and the human evidence is indirect.

The consensus is that immune and infectious factors contribute to risk in some cases, alongside strong genetic and environmental contributions, and that no single trigger has been identified.

Where it stands

Documented: associations with prenatal infection and immune genes.

Documented: animal models of maternal immune activation.

Small: the effect sizes.

Contested: the causal pathway in humans.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real association that is often reported as a cause, and because the difference matters for how the illness is understood.
