The abc Conjecture

A conjecture about the prime factors of a+b=c that would settle a family of Diophantine problems. A claimed proof has been disputed since 2012.

The statement

For coprime positive integers a, b, c with a+b=c, the abc conjecture bounds c in terms of the product of the distinct prime factors of abc, raised to a power slightly greater than one. It was proposed by Oesterlé and Masser in 1985.

Maps, diagrams and images

Why it matters

The conjecture implies many results, including versions of Fermat's Last Theorem for large exponents, and it constrains Diophantine equations generally. It is one of the most consequential open problems in number theory.

In 2012 Shinichi Mochizuki published a proof running to hundreds of pages and introducing an entirely new framework, inter-universal Teichmüller theory. Years of scrutiny by specialists have not produced agreement: the proof has been published in a journal edited by Mochizuki, and most of the field does not accept it as verified.

Where it stands

Proposed: 1985.

Consequences: many, across number theory.

Claimed proof: Mochizuki, 2012.

Status of the proof: disputed; not accepted by most specialists.

The label: open problem.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the mathematics is real and the dispute is about whether a proof can be understood — which is unusual enough to be worth recording.
