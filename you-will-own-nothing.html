You Will Own Nothing

A phrase from a marketing essay, read as a policy programme. The essay is real; the programme is not.

The phrase

A 2016 World Economic Forum essay by an advertising executive predicted that by 2030 'you will own nothing and you will be happy', describing a trend towards access over ownership in consumer markets. The phrase became a slogan for claims about a planned abolition of property.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The essay was a marketing prediction by a guest author, not a policy document; the Forum does not set law, and no government has adopted such a programme. The claim treats a corporate foresight piece as a plan.

The archive files it as debunked.

What is established

Documented: the essay and its author.

Documented: the phrase's spread.

Not established: any policy programme.

The error: a prediction read as a plan.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the quotation is accurate and the conclusion is not.
