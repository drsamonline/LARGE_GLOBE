Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer

A conjecture connecting the number of rational points on an elliptic curve to a function attached to it. It is the Millennium problem with the most computational support.

The conjecture

For an elliptic curve — a curve of the form y² = x³ + ax + b — the set of rational points can be finite or infinite, and its structure is described by its rank. The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture relates that rank to the behaviour of an associated L-function at a single point: the rank should equal the order of the zero of the L-function there.

The evidence

The conjecture is supported by enormous computation and by partial results: it is known in some cases, including for curves of rank 0 and 1 under certain conditions, and it is one of the Millennium Prize problems.

It connects two objects that look unrelated — a geometric count and an analytic function — which is why it is regarded as deep rather than merely hard.

Where it stands

Formulated: 1965.

Proved: in special cases.

Supported: by extensive computation.

Prize: Millennium Prize.

The label: open problem.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the open problem where the numerical evidence is strongest, and because that has not been enough for sixty years.
