Reading keyFour labels, used consistently

Documented

Primary documents, peer review or official records. Example: the CIA ran MKUltra.

Live debate

Argued in the literature, usually at the margins. Example: slow Earth expansion.

Fringe claim

Widely circulated, no accepted evidence, no working mechanism. Example: a habitable inner sun.

Debunked / hoax

Tested and falsified, or admitted fabricated. Example: the 1934 “surgeon’s photograph”.

The family treeHow the theories feed each other

These are not separate beliefs. The ice wall is the rim of the flat disc and the entrance to the hollow shell; the inner sun lights the interior where the giants and living dinosaurs went; Tartarus is the mythological name pasted onto the same pit.

Solid lines: direct lineage in the literature. Dashed: thematic borrowing — the same image reused for a different argument.

MethodWhy this archive exists in this shape

A hidden-world claim usually arrives in one of two forms: a screenshot with no author, or a ninety-minute video with no citations. Both are impossible to check. This archive rebuilds each idea from the bottom up — find who said it first, read what they actually wrote, then follow the chain forward to whoever tested it.

That is also why the tone is flat. The material is genuinely interesting: Wegener’s continental drift was ridiculed for fifty years before plate tectonics vindicated it, and the expanding-Earth school grew out of the same jigsaw fit everyone now accepts. Knowing why one survived and the other did not is more useful than knowing which is currently official.

One rule runs through every page: a claim and its evidence are different things. The archive tells you what was claimed, by whom, in what year, and what the measurement said. It does not tell you what to conclude.
