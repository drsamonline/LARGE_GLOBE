The Numbers

Four charts and a method diagram, generated from the archive itself: verdicts, sourcing, shelf sizes and picture rights.

What this page is for

Numbers are a fast honesty check on a publication. These four charts are generated from the archive's own content every time the site is rebuilt: the verdicts, themes and source counts come from `data/claims.json`, and the licences from the credits page. The generator is `tools/rebuild-charts.py`, and CI fails if this page drifts from either. Nothing here is typed by hand.

They also show the limits. This is an editorial collection, not a survey: the labels are judgements about evidence, made by one archive, and the counts describe what has been written so far rather than what exists.

Where the archive lands

Two populations, two charts, and they are not supposed to agree. The master list catalogues 150 claims; the archive holds 1010 dossiers. A claim can be catalogued as fringe and written up as a live debate once the argument turns out to be closer than it looked, and the reverse happens too. The point of both charts is not that most claims fail — it is that the labels are used at all, including on comfortable claims.

Keep the archive open

No ads, no accounts, no tracking, nothing for sale. Hosting, image licensing and the reading behind every dossier are paid out of pocket. If the archive earned its keep, you can put something back.

Nothing is gated behind it — every dossier stays free, unpaywalled and readable offline.
