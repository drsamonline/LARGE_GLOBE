The Elisa Lam Tapes

A student died in a Los Angeles hotel in 2013, and the elevator footage of her last hours became one of the internet's most analysed videos. The official finding is an accidental drowning; the theories have not stopped.

The case

Elisa Lam, a Canadian student, was found dead in a water tank on the roof of the Cecil Hotel in February 2013, nineteen days after she was reported missing. The coroner recorded accidental drowning, with bipolar disorder as a contributing factor.

Maps, diagrams and images

The elevator footage released by police shows her entering, pressing buttons, stepping out and back, and making unusual gestures. It became the most analysed video of its kind, with theories ranging from foul play to supernatural explanations.

The explanations

The mundane account fits the record: a young woman in a psychiatric crisis, behaving in ways consistent with it; a hotel with a history of poor security; a roof access that was not secured; a water tank that was accessible. The toxicology and the coroner's finding are on the record.

The 'strangeness' of the footage is largely the strangeness of watching someone in distress without context, in a low frame-rate clip that was edited for release.

Where it stands

Documented: the death, the coroner's finding, the footage, the hotel's condition.

Documented: a history of safety problems at the hotel.

Not established: foul play; no evidence has been produced.

Not established: any paranormal element.

The honest label: live debate in public, but a closed case in law, and the archive files it as a case where the internet's need for a mystery outlasted the evidence.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the archetype of the internet true-crime case: a real death, a strange video, a documented explanation, and an audience that prefers the version without one.
