Fusion on the Grid

In December 2022 the National Ignition Facility got more energy out of a fusion target than the laser delivered. Headlines said fusion had arrived. The grid is still waiting.

What was achieved

NIF's laser delivered about 2.05 megajoules to a tiny fuel capsule and the reaction released about 3.15 megajoules — a target gain above one, and a genuine first. It has since been repeated with higher yields.

The achievement is scientific: it shows that a burning plasma can be made to release more energy than was deposited in it, which is the central physics question for inertial confinement.

What was not

The laser itself drew roughly 300 megajoules from the grid to produce those 2 megajoules of laser light. Wall-plug gain is therefore far below one, and NIF was built as a weapons-physics facility, not a power plant.

Magnetic confinement faces different problems: ITER, the large international tokamak, has slipped its schedule and its first plasma is now expected in the 2030s. Private companies promise pilot plants sooner; several have plausible physics and unproven engineering.

The engineering problems that remain

Tritium. A fusion plant must breed its own fuel from lithium. No closed fuel cycle has been demonstrated at power-plant scale.

Materials. Structural components face neutron fluxes far beyond fission experience, and must last years.

Availability. A power station must run most of the year. Present experiments run for seconds to minutes.

Cost. Even successful fusion competes with solar, wind and storage that have fallen in price by orders of magnitude.

The archive's position: the physics milestone is documented and real; 'fusion power within a decade' is a claim that has been made since the 1950s and remains a forecast.

Why it is in the archive

Because fusion is the cleanest example of a genuine breakthrough reported as a solved problem, and because both the hype and the cynical dismissal miss the actual state of the field.

Related: the Jevons paradox for what happens when energy gets cheaper.
