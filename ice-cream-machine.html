The Ice Cream Machine

McDonald's ice cream machines break constantly, and it is a franchise-wide joke. The machines are made by one company, their error codes are proprietary, and the repair rules are the story.

The phenomenon

The soft-serve machines in McDonald's restaurants are manufactured by Taylor, and they are notoriously prone to being out of service. The machines require a nightly pasteurisation cycle, they throw error codes that staff cannot diagnose, and repairs are restricted to authorised technicians.

Maps, diagrams and images

The argument

In 2020 a company built a device to read the machines' diagnostic data, and was sued; the dispute became a test case for the right to repair. Investigations found that many outages are caused by cleaning cycles and minor faults that staff could fix if the codes were documented.

The counter-argument is that the machines are food-safety equipment with real constraints, and that unauthorised repair carries genuine risk. Both things are true, which is why the topic is filed as a live debate rather than a scandal.

Where it stands

Documented: the failure rate, the error codes and the repair restrictions.

Documented: the right-to-repair dispute.

Contested: how much of the downtime is avoidable.

Contested: the safety argument for the restrictions.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most ordinary possible example of a systemic problem — and because a joke that everyone makes turned out to have a documented cause.
