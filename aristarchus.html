Aristarchus Put the Sun at the Centre

A Greek astronomer proposed a heliocentric system eighteen centuries before Copernicus. The report survives, and the idea was rejected.

The proposal

Aristarchus of Samos, in the third century BC, proposed that the Sun is at the centre and the Earth orbits it, and estimated the Sun's distance and size. The proposal is reported by Archimedes and by later writers, and his surviving treatise on sizes and distances uses geometric methods.

Maps, diagrams and images

The reception

The idea was rejected by contemporaries and successors, largely because it required the Earth to move and because no stellar parallax was observed. It survived as a reported curiosity rather than a tradition, and Copernicus does not appear to have known it directly.

The archive files it as documented because the report is in Archimedes and the geometry is in the treatise.

What is established

Documented: the proposal, via Archimedes.

Documented: the surviving treatise.

Documented: its rejection.

Not a tradition: it did not carry forward.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it shows that having the right idea is not the same as having it accepted.
