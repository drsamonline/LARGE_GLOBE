Dwarka

Ruins have been found under the sea off Gujarat, and they are real. The claim that they are the 32,000-year-old city of Krishna is not supported by their dating.

The finds

Marine archaeologists have recorded submerged structures off Dwarka in Gujarat since the 1980s: wall segments, a possible harbour, anchors, pottery and stone objects. The finds are documented in the archaeological literature and are the subject of ongoing survey.

Maps, diagrams and images

The claims

The claim that these are the city of Krishna described in the Mahabharata, dated by some to more than 30,000 years ago, is not supported by the material. The pottery and structures are consistent with historical periods, and the surviving ruins have been associated with the historical Dwarka of the first millennium BC or later.

The submerged structures are also partly natural: some of the recorded 'walls' have been interpreted as beachrock, a sedimentary formation that can look artificial.

Where it stands

Documented: submerged structures, anchors and pottery.

Documented: the archaeological surveys.

Contested: the dating and the identification with the legendary city.

Not supported: the 30,000-year date.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real underwater site with a legend attached, and because the dating is the whole question.
