Tardigrades Between Planets

A millimetre-long animal that survives vacuum, radiation and decades without water is real. The claim built on top of it — that life travels between planets — is a different proposition, and the experiments that get quoted do not support it.

The animal

Tardigrades were described in 1773 by Johann August Ephraim Goeze, who called them kleiner Wasserbär — little water bear. They are eight-legged animals a fraction of a millimetre long, and there are more than a thousand described species. They live in moss, soil, lake sediment and the sea, which is unremarkable. What made them famous is what happens when the water goes.

Cryptobiosis

A desiccated tardigrade contracts into a tun: a barrel-shaped state in which metabolism falls below the level anyone can measure, the cell replaces water with trehalose and specific proteins, and DNA damage is repaired on rehydration. In that state they have survived years of vacuum, temperatures from near absolute zero to above 100 °C, pressures of hundreds of megapascals, and doses of ionising radiation hundreds of times what would kill a human.

Two results get quoted more than the rest. In 2007, a European team launched dehydrated tardigrades into low Earth orbit for ten days; animals exposed to vacuum and solar radiation survived, and some reproduced after return. In 2019, an Israeli lunar lander carrying a library of human DNA and dried tardigrades crashed on the Moon — which produced headlines about life on the Moon, and no evidence of it.

What the evidence supports

Documented: cryptobiosis, and survival of space vacuum and radiation in the tun state, demonstrated in orbit.

Not shown: that tardigrades are active or metabolising in space. The survival is of a dormant state, not of a living animal doing anything.

Not shown: panspermia. That life could survive a journey says nothing about whether it made one, and the radiation dose over a multi-year transit inside a rock is far above what the orbit experiment tested.

Retracted, not repeated: the 2015 claim that a sixth of the tardigrade genome is bacterial, from horizontal transfer. Re-analysis in 2016 found contamination, and the figure collapsed to about one percent.

Why the claim survives the evidence

Because the real animal is extraordinary enough that the exaggeration is unnecessary. A creature that can be dried to powder, kept in a drawer for years, and then rehydrated and walk away is a better story than one that colonises planets, and it has the advantage of being true.

The archive keeps this page because it is the cleanest example of a pattern worth recognising: a real, measured, remarkable capability, with an unmeasured inference attached to it. The capability is documented. The inference is not.
