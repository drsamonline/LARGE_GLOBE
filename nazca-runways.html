Nazca Lines as Runways

The lines and figures on the Nazca desert were said to be landing strips for aircraft. They are shallow scrapes in the surface, and the ground beneath them would not support a wheel.

The claim

Erich von Däniken's 1968 book argued that the Nazca lines were runways for spacecraft. The argument rests on their size, their straightness, and their visibility from the air.

Maps, diagrams and images

The archaeology

The lines were made between roughly 500 BC and 500 AD by removing the dark iron-oxide-coated surface stones to expose the lighter ground beneath. The scrapes are shallow — a few centimetres — and the desert's climate has preserved them. The figures include animals and plants, and the lines are associated with water and with processional routes; many converge on places where water is available.

The 'runway' claim fails on engineering: the surface is soft, the lines are not level, some run into hills, and there is no evidence of any landing gear, fuel or structure.

What is established

Documented: the construction method and the dates.

Documented: the association with water and ritual movement.

Not established: any runway, aircraft or vehicle.

Not plausible: the surface would not support a landing.

The honest label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most famous ancient-astronaut claim, and because the archaeology is more interesting than the runway.
