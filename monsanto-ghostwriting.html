Monsanto's Ghostwritten Studies

Internal documents showed the company drafting research on its own products and arranging for academics to publish it.

The documents

Documents released during litigation in 2015 showed that Monsanto staff drafted studies and review articles about the safety of glyphosate and arranged for outside academics to be named as authors, with editorial control retained by the company.

Maps, diagrams and images

The significance

The practice — ghostwriting for scientific publication — is not unique to one company, but the documents are unusually explicit. It matters because the resulting papers were cited as independent evidence, and because the reviewers' names carried academic authority.

The archive files it as documented because the internal correspondence is on the record.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the clearest evidence that a published paper is not automatically an independent finding.
