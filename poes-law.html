Poe's Law

Without a clear marker of intent, a parody of extremism is indistinguishable from the real thing. It was formulated on a forum and has been demonstrated repeatedly since.

The law

Poe's law states that without a clear indicator of the author's intent, it is impossible to tell the difference between a parody of extremism and the real thing. It was formulated on the Christian Forums site around 2005 by Nathan Poe, in the context of creationist arguments.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

The demonstrations are abundant. Sincere believers have adopted slogans invented as parody; parody accounts have been taken as genuine by large audiences; and the 'flat earth' and 'birds aren't real' movements each contain participants who are joking and participants who are not, with no reliable way to tell from the content.

The law is a statement about interpretation under conditions of low information, and it explains why the archive insists on provenance: who said it, where, and with what record.

What is established

Documented: the formulation and its origin.

Documented: repeated demonstrations.

The mechanism: absence of intent markers.

Not a law of nature: a heuristic about interpretation.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the reason this archive labels fiction as fiction, and because a reader who does not know it will be fooled by design.
