The Modern 200

Two hundred modern theories — machine minds, the collapsing open web, fresh physics, fan lore and scandals that turned out to be real — each labelled with what the evidence says.

How to read this index

The companion to The 200, and built the same way: one line per claim, the archive's evidence label, and a link where a full dossier already exists. Nothing here is a substitute for the dossier — it is a map of what is worth reading first.

The labels: Documented — the thing exists or happened, on the record. Live debate — real evidence, real specialists, no settled answer. Fringe — no positive evidence, often unfalsifiable in practice. Debunked — tested and failed, or a known hoax. Fiction — invented story, fan theory or internet lore, labelled as such rather than as a claim about the world.

What this collection gets wrong

This list is more careful than most, but nine entries still need correcting, and the index marks them rather than repeating the framing:

  • Satoshi as an intelligence agency has no evidence of any kind. The Bitcoin design and the surviving writings have been read closely for fifteen years; nothing ties them to an agency.
  • The Titanic–Olympic switch is refuted by the wreck itself: stamped "401" parts, the correct yard number, sit on the seabed. Olympic sailed on until 1935.
  • Stevie Wonder is blind — retinopathy of prematurity, since infancy. The clips show a man who hears the room, not a man who sees it.
  • 5G and COVID has no mechanism, and major outbreaks arrived in places before their networks did.
  • "You will own nothing" is a sentence from a 2016 speculative essay by Danish politician Ida Auken, written as an imagined 2030, not a WEF policy.
  • The Taylor Swift clone claim rests on facial resemblance to Zeena Schreck, who has publicly denied it; there is no other basis.
  • Randonautica uses ordinary random coordinates. Random number generators are not influenced by intention; the "anomalies" are selection bias plus a walk.
  • Momo was never found in a single video. The sculpture is by Keisuke Aiso, and UK police and charities found no cases.
  • The man from Taured and Andrew Carlssin have no primary record at all — both trace to later retellings, one of them a tabloid humour column.

Two entries in this list are also stronger than stated: the Fallout Vault-Tec theory and the GTA universe split are no longer theories, they are canon, and the index labels them Documented for that reason.
