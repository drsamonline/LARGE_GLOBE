Forensic fMRI

The claim that brain scans can detect lies or memories. The research exists; the courtroom use does not.

The claims

Companies and researchers have proposed using fMRI to detect deception or to determine whether a defendant recognises a stimulus, and the technique has been offered commercially in some countries.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

Studies show above-chance but unreliable performance, vulnerability to countermeasures, and a gap between laboratory conditions and real cases. Courts have largely excluded it, and the scientific consensus is documented as negative for forensic use.

What is established

Documented: the research.

Above chance: in laboratory settings.

Not reliable: for forensic use.

Excluded: by most courts.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because a brain image looks like evidence, and looking like evidence is not the same as being it.
