About

What this archive is, and what it is not.

A reference library of hidden-world claims: what was claimed, who claimed it, what the evidence shows, and what label the claim has earned. Every page names its sources. Nothing here is for sale.

The method

Each dossier does the same five things. It states the claim in the claimant's own terms. It names the person or movement that made it. It shows the evidence, including the evidence that fails. It records what happened when the claim was tested. And it gives the claim a label.

Every dossier carries two things: a verdict and a note. The verdict is one of six, and it is what the filters, the charts and the sorting use — Documented means the events happened and the record supports the account; Debunked means the claim was testable and failed; Live debate means serious people disagree and the disagreement is documented; Fringe claim means the evidence is absent rather than contradicted; Fiction means nobody is claiming it happened; Open problem means the question is real and unsolved.

Where six words cannot carry the finding, the page adds a note in place of the label — Two centuries, no treasure for Oak Island, Ended by DNA for the Anastasia claim, Geology, not architecture for the Bosnian pyramids. The note is written by hand and used once; the verdict behind it is one of the six, printed in the panel at the top of the page and carried in the badge's tooltip. A reader can therefore sort every page by verdict without losing the sentence that says what actually happened.

Sources

Every dossier links its sources, and the count is printed on the page. The archive prefers primary documents, peer-reviewed literature and institutional records, and it says when a source is a secondary summary. Where a claim rests on testimony alone, the page says so.

Images

Every photograph comes from Wikimedia Commons under a free licence, and every one carries its author, its licence and a link to the source file. Diagrams are drawn for the archive and are credited as such. Nothing is hotlinked, nothing is uncredited, and images are stored locally so the archive still reads offline. The full list is on the image credits page.

Privacy

The site sets no cookie, loads no third-party script and runs no analytics. Two preferences — theme and text size — are stored in your own browser and never transmitted. The full statement, with the commands that verify each claim, is in PRIVACY.md; the licence position for every photograph is in NOTICE.md, and the limits of the archive's authority are in DISCLAIMER.md.

Money

There are no ads, no accounts, no trackers and no paywall. Hosting, image licensing and the reading behind each dossier are paid out of pocket. If you want to put something back, the support page lists the ways, and none of them buys influence over a page.

Corrections

The full history of changes is in CHANGELOG.md, generated from the repository's own commits.

Pages change when the evidence changes. Every correction is logged on the corrections page with what was wrong and what replaced it. If you find an error, the correction is the point of the exercise rather than an embarrassment.

What the archive will not do

It will not tell you what to think about a live political argument. It will not present a photograph of one thing as evidence of another. It will not draw a map of a place that does not exist. And it will not label a claim more kindly than the evidence allows, or more harshly.

Who makes it
