The Rainbow Warrior

French intelligence officers sank a Greenpeace ship in Auckland harbour, killing a photographer. France initially denied it, then admitted it.

The attack

On 10 July 1985, two explosive charges sank the Greenpeace ship Rainbow Warrior in Auckland harbour, where it was preparing to sail to protest against French nuclear testing at Moruroa. The photographer Fernando Pereira was killed.

Maps, diagrams and images

The exposure

New Zealand police arrested two French nationals travelling on false Swiss passports; they pleaded guilty to manslaughter and were sentenced, then held on an island before being repatriated. Investigative journalism established that the operation was authorised by the French defence ministry and the DGSE, and that the president knew.

France denied responsibility at first, then admitted it after the evidence became overwhelming; the defence minister resigned. A subsequent French inquiry exonerated the officers involved.

What is established

Documented: the attack, the death, the arrests and the convictions.

Documented: the authorisation at ministerial level.

Documented: the initial denial and later admission.

Not established: some details of the chain of command.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a state conspiracy proven by court record, and because the cover-up lasted weeks, not decades.
