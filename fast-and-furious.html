Operation Fast and Furious

US agents allowed firearms to be trafficked into Mexico in order to trace them, and lost track of most of them. Two were found at the scene of a Border Patrol agent's killing.

The operation

Operation Fast and Furious was run by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives between 2009 and 2011 in Arizona. Agents allowed suspected straw purchasers to buy firearms and cross into Mexico, in the hope of tracing the weapons to cartel leaders rather than arresting buyers at the border.

Maps, diagrams and images

The consequences

Around 2,000 firearms were involved, and most were not recovered. Two were found at the scene of the killing of Border Patrol agent Brian Terry in 2010, which brought the operation into public view. Congressional investigations found serious failures of oversight, and the ATF's acting director was reassigned; the attorney general was held in contempt of Congress over documents.

The operation's defenders argue that it was a flawed attempt at a hard problem; its critics that it armed criminals with official sanction.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the conspiracy theory — the government let guns walk — turned out to be substantially true, and was admitted.
