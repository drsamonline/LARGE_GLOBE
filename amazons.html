The Amazons

Greek writers described warrior women. Archaeology has found women buried with weapons on the steppe.

The evidence

Excavations of Scythian and Sarmatian burials on the Eurasian steppe have found women buried with weapons, armour and horse gear, in numbers that imply real female warriors. The finds are documented in burial reports across Russia, Ukraine and Kazakhstan.

The myth

Greek writers described the Amazons as a nation of warrior women; the archaeological record shows that the reality behind the stories was probably mounted warriors of the steppe, of both sexes. The myth's details — a separate society, the removal of a breast — are literary.

The archive files it as documented because the burials are the evidence.

What is established

Documented: female burials with weapons.

Documented: the steppe cultures.

Documented: Greek literary accounts.

Literary: the myth's details.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where the myth turned out to have a real basis, and the basis is better.
