John Titor

A forum poster in 2000 claimed to be a time traveller from 2036. His predictions failed, his physics did not work, and the posts have been traced to a group of people.

The posts

Between 2000 and 2001, a poster calling himself John Titor appeared on several internet forums, claiming to be a soldier from 2036 sent back to retrieve an IBM computer for a legacy system. He described a civil war in the United States, a changed constitution, and a device for creating miniature black holes. He also posted photographs of what he said was his time machine.

Maps, diagrams and images

The failures

The predictions were specific and did not happen: no civil war in 2004–2015, no constitutional change of the kind described. The physics — a portable micro-singularity generator — is not consistent with known physics, and the photographs show an ordinary appliance.

Later investigation traced the posts to a group of people, one of whom was connected to a legal dispute over the character. That is a mundane explanation, and the failure of the dated predictions is independent of it.

What is established

Documented: the posts, their dates, and their content.

Documented: the specific predictions, and their failure.

Documented: the physical implausibility of the described device.

Likely: an origin in a small group of people, not a time traveller.

The lesson: dated predictions are checkable, and this is what happens when they are checked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the internet's founding time-travel story, and because it is a clean case of a claim that gave itself a deadline and missed it.
