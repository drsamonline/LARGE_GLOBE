FIFA Bribes

Senior football officials were indicted in the United States for racketeering and bribery over media and marketing rights. The president resigned.

The indictments

In May 2015 the US Department of Justice indicted fourteen people, including senior FIFA officials, on charges of racketeering, wire fraud and money laundering, over decades of bribes connected to the sale of media and marketing rights for football tournaments in the Americas.

Maps, diagrams and images

The aftermath

Sepp Blatter resigned as president days after the arrests, and FIFA's ethics committee banned him from the sport for eight years (later reduced). Several officials were convicted, and the organisation was restructured under a new president. Swiss prosecutors also investigated the award of the 2018 and 2022 World Cups; a criminal case over the 2006 German bid collapsed in 2021.

The evidence base is court filings and plea agreements, which is what makes this page a documented case rather than a rumour about a governing body.

What is established

Documented: the indictments, the pleas and the convictions.

Documented: the resignation and the bans.

Documented: the scale of the bribery scheme.

Contested: the extent of corruption in the World Cup awards.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the conspiracy was real, the paper trail was in another country, and the sport's own governance could not have found it.
