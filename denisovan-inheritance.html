Denisovan Inheritance

A hominin known from a finger bone, whose genes are in living people from Tibet to Melanesia.

The find

In 2010 a finger bone from Denisova Cave in Siberia yielded DNA from a previously unknown hominin, named the Denisovans. Later finds included teeth and a jaw, and the genome showed interbreeding with modern humans.

Maps, diagrams and images

The legacy

Denisovan DNA is found in living populations across Asia and Oceania, at the highest levels in Melanesia. A variant of the EPAS1 gene, inherited from Denisovans, helps Tibetans cope with low oxygen — the clearest case of an extinct hominin's gene doing something measurable in living people.

The archive files it as documented because the genome and the inheritance are published and replicated.

What is established

Documented: the genome from a finger bone.

Documented: interbreeding with modern humans.

Documented: EPAS1 and high-altitude adaptation.

Documented: the Melanesian signal.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the strongest case of an extinct relative still present in living bodies.
