The Chimera

A lion with a goat's head and a serpent's tail, and a possible origin in volcanic landscape.

The creature

The Chimera appears in Homer and in art as a composite beast, and it is placed in Lycia. Explanations have included a volcano with a burning crater and snake-infested slopes, a fossil, and a purely literary invention.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

The volcanic explanation fits the region's geography — the Yanartaş gas vents burn continuously — and it is a suggestion rather than a documented link. Composite creatures are common in ancient art, which weakens the need for a specific source.

Where it stands

Documented: the myth and the geography.

Plausible: the volcanic reading.

Not established: any link.

Common: composite creatures in art.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a monster that may be a landscape, and the landscape is still burning.
