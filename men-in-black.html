The Men in Black

Men in dark suits who visit witnesses and warn them to keep quiet. The stories are consistent, old, and unsupported by a single verified case.

The stories

The Men in Black entered UFO folklore in the 1950s: witnesses reported being visited by men in dark suits, sometimes in groups of three, who warned them to stop talking, produced official-looking identification, and behaved oddly. The most-cited early case is Albert Bender's, who later retracted his account.

Maps, diagrams and images

The record

No visit has been verified, and the descriptions are inconsistent in detail while consistent in outline — the pattern of a legend rather than of an event. The folklore was shaped by the writer Gray Barker's 1956 book, which is where the image of the men in black suits was fixed in the literature.

Real government agents do visit people, and the air force did have officers interview witnesses; that mundane reality is enough to seed the legend.

Where it stands

Documented: the stories and their literary origin.

Documented: real interviews by real officials.

Not established: any organised intimidation programme.

Not established: any single verified visit.

The honest label: fringe.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where the folklore has a dated, named origin, and the origin is a book.
