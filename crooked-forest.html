The Crooked Forest

About four hundred pines near Gryfino in Poland bend sharply north at their base. They were planted in the 1930s, and nobody knows why they bend.

The trees

The Crooked Forest near Gryfino in western Poland contains roughly 400 pines, planted in the 1930s, whose trunks bend sharply to the north at about 20 cm above the ground, then straighten. The bend is at the same height and in the same direction in every tree, which suggests a single cause applied to all of them.

Maps, diagrams and images

The explanations

The leading suggestion is human intervention: the trees were deliberately bent, perhaps for furniture, boat ribs or barrel staves, using a technique that involves damaging or weighting the young leader. The forest was planted in the 1930s, when the region was German, and the trees were never harvested.

Other explanations — heavy snow, a landslide, grazing animals — struggle with the uniformity of direction and height, though none is excluded. No documentation of the planting has been found.

Where it stands

Documented: the trees, their age, the uniformity of the bend.

Most likely: deliberate human shaping.

Contested: the purpose, and whether it was deliberate at all.

Not documented: the planting or the technique.

The honest label: live debate, with a good answer and no record.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a genuinely unexplained thing that does not need an exotic explanation, and because the most likely answer is a forgotten craft.
