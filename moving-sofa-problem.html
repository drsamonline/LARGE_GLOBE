The Moving Sofa Problem

The largest sofa that can be moved round a right-angled corridor. The best known answer has stood since 1992 and is not proved optimal.

The problem

Formulated by Leo Moser in 1966: what is the largest area of a sofa that can be manoeuvred around a right-angled corner in a corridor of unit width? The problem is a classic of recreational geometry and a genuine open problem in analysis.

Maps, diagrams and images

The bounds

John Hammersley found a shape of area about 2.2074 in 1968; Joseph Gerver found a better one, about 2.2195, in 1992, and conjectured it optimal. A 2017 result proved that Gerver's sofa is locally optimal, and later work has narrowed the upper bound, but the exact answer is unknown.

The problem is unusual in that the answer is a specific number nobody can compute.

Where it stands

Best known: Gerver's sofa, about 2.2195.

Proved: locally optimal, 2017.

Open: global optimality.

Bounds: narrowed, not closed.

The label: open problem.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most accessible open problem in mathematics, and because accessibility is not the same as ease.
