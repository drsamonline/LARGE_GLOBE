Digital-Twin Predictive Policing

The claim that cities will be policed through real-time simulations of their populations. The components exist; the system does not.

The claim

The claim is that predictive policing will evolve into a 'digital twin' of a city — a real-time simulation fed by cameras, sensors, phones and records, used to anticipate crime and deploy officers. The components exist: predictive policing software has been sold and used, and cities run sensor networks.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Predictive policing systems have been deployed and evaluated, with mixed results and documented problems of bias and feedback. No city runs a comprehensive real-time digital twin of its population, and the computing and data requirements for one are beyond current practice.

The claim is a projection from existing components rather than a description of a system.

Where it stands

Documented: predictive policing systems and sensor networks.

Documented: their mixed results and bias problems.

Not deployed: a comprehensive digital twin.

Projected: by vendors and researchers.

The honest label: fringe.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a claim assembled from real parts, and because the parts are less capable than the whole is said to be.
