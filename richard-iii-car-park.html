Richard III Under a Car Park

The last Plantagenet king was buried in a friary that became a car park. His skeleton was found in 2012, under a marked parking bay.

The search

Richard III died at Bosworth in 1485 and was buried in the choir of the Grey Friars church in Leicester. The church was dissolved and demolished, and the site became a car park. In 2012 a project led by the University of Leicester and the Richard III Society excavated the site and found a skeleton in a hastily dug grave.

Maps, diagrams and images

The identification

The skeleton showed a curved spine consistent with contemporary descriptions, battle injuries, and — critically — mitochondrial DNA matched to living maternal-line descendants. The identification was published in 2014, and the remains were reinterred in Leicester Cathedral in 2015.

The project is one of the few cases where a historical identification was made and confirmed by genetic evidence.

What is established

Documented: the burial place and the excavation.

Documented: the DNA match.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the best modern example of an identification being settled rather than argued, and because the answer was under a parking space.
