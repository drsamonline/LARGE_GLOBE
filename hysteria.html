Hysteria

A diagnosis applied to women for two thousand years, and removed from the manuals in 1980.

The diagnosis

Hysteria was diagnosed from antiquity into the twentieth century, overwhelmingly in women, with symptoms ranging from fainting to paralysis. Charcot's demonstrations at the Salpêtrière made it a public spectacle, and Freud built early psychoanalysis on it.

Maps, diagrams and images

The removal

The category was dropped from the DSM in 1980, its symptoms redistributed into other diagnoses including conversion disorder. Historians and clinicians document it as a case of a diagnosis shaped by the expectations of its era.

What is established

Documented: the diagnosis and its history.

Documented: its removal in 1980.

Not a disease: as described.

Reshaped: into other categories.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the clearest case of a diagnosis that said more about the doctor than the patient.
