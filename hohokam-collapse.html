The Hohokam Collapse

A desert society built hundreds of kilometres of canals, then abandoned them. The cause is argued.

The achievement

The Hohokam of the Salt and Gila river valleys in Arizona built an extensive canal system, some of it kilometres long, to irrigate fields in the desert. The canals are documented by survey and some are still visible.

Maps, diagrams and images

The collapse

By around 1450 the system was abandoned and the settlements dispersed. Proposed causes include drought, flooding that destroyed canals, salinisation of soils, and social disruption. The evidence supports several factors rather than one.

The archive files it as live debate because the abandonment is documented and the cause is not settled.

Where it stands

Documented: the canal system.

Documented: the abandonment.

Contested: the cause.

Multiple factors: supported.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a real hydraulic civilisation whose end is a warning rather than a mystery.
