Hair and Nails After Death

Hair and nails do not grow after death. The skin recedes and creates the illusion.

The claim

The claim that hair and nails continue to grow after death is common and appears in literature and forensic folklore.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Growth requires metabolism, which stops at death. What happens is that the skin dehydrates and retracts, exposing more of the hair shaft and nail, which makes them appear longer. The effect is documented in forensic literature.

What is established

Documented: the claim.

Documented: skin retraction.

Not real: post-mortem growth.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is an illusion with a mechanism, which is the archive's favourite kind of error.
