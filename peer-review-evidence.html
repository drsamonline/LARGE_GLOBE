Peer Review's Evidence Base

Peer review is the gatekeeping ritual of science, and the evidence that it works is thinner than most people assume.

The evidence

Studies of peer review have found modest effects on manuscript quality, inconsistency between reviewers, and a bias towards famous authors and institutions. Trials that randomised review — including the BMJ's — found that masking authors made little difference.

Maps, diagrams and images

The debate

Defenders argue that review's function is gatekeeping and improvement rather than detection of fraud, and that alternatives have their own problems. Critics argue that its effectiveness is assumed rather than demonstrated.
