Microbiome Forensics

The bacteria on a body or a phone may identify a person or a place. The research is early.

The idea

Microbial communities on skin, in the gut and on surfaces vary between individuals and change after death, which suggests uses in identifying contacts, estimating time since death, and linking objects to people.

Maps, diagrams and images

The state of the field

Studies show that microbiome profiles can distinguish individuals and that post-mortem communities change predictably, and the error rates and stability needed for court are not established. The research is documented as promising and early.

Where it stands

Documented: individual variation.

Documented: post-mortem change.

Not established: forensic reliability.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the next forensic method, and the archive prefers to label it before a court does.
