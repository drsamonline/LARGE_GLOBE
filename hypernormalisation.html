Hypernormalisation

Adam Curtis's argument that politics has become a managed spectacle. It is a documentary essay, and it is argued as one.

The argument

Adam Curtis's 2016 film argues that governments and corporations, unable to manage a complex world, have created a simplified version of reality that everyone pretends is real — 'hypernormalisation'. It draws a line from Cold War think tanks to social media.

Maps, diagrams and images

The problems

The film is an essay, assembled from archive footage and a narration that asserts connections rather than demonstrating them. Historians have criticised specific claims and the compression of decades into a single thesis. It is influential and it is not scholarship.

The archive files it as live debate.

Where it stands

Documented: the film and its argument.

Documented: its influence.

Contested: specific historical claims.

Essayistic: in method.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most-watched argument about manufactured reality, and the method is the thing to examine.
