Microplastics and Hormones

Microplastics are everywhere, including in blood and placentas. Whether they act as endocrine disruptors in people is a question the exposure data cannot yet answer.

What is established

Microplastics — fragments under five millimetres — have been found in oceans, soils, air, bottled water, human blood, lung tissue and placentas. The measurement methods have improved sharply since 2004, and the exposure is now well documented.

Maps, diagrams and images

What is contested

Some plastics contain or leach chemicals that are endocrine-active in laboratory assays — bisphenols, phthalates, and additives. The question is whether the doses that reach human tissue produce effects. The evidence is mostly from cell and animal studies at concentrations above environmental levels, plus epidemiological associations that cannot establish causation.

The honest position is that the exposure is real, the mechanism is plausible, and the human effect size is unknown.

Where it stands

Documented: widespread human exposure.

Documented: endocrine activity of some plastic chemicals in the lab.

Contested: whether environmental doses produce human effects.

Not established: a causal link to hormonal disease.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where the exposure is undeniable and the consequence is unknown, and most writing collapses the two.
