The Nazca Lines as Water Maps

The lines may mark routes to water rather than sky gods or runways. The puquios — spiral wells — are real, and the lines' relationship to them is argued.

The hypothesis

The water hypothesis holds that the Nazca lines and geoglyphs were built by a society managing scarce water: the lines mark paths to water sources, relate to underground aqueducts called puquios, and were used in rituals concerned with water and fertility.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

The puquios are real and were built by the Nazca culture, and several studies have found that the lines and the water sources are related spatially. The lines are also associated with processional routes, and some geoglyphs are animal figures that may relate to water and fertility.

The hypothesis does not explain every line, and the debate about the purpose — ritual, water, astronomy, or all three — is unresolved.

Where it stands

Documented: the puquios and their builders.

Documented: spatial relationships between lines and water.

Contested: the purpose of the lines.

Not established: a single function.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the most plausible account of the lines and it is still not the whole answer.
