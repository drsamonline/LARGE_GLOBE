The Somerton Man

A man found dead on an Adelaide beach in 1948, with a scrap of Persian poetry and an unbreakable code. He was identified by DNA in 2022.

The case

In December 1948 a man was found dead on Somerton Beach in Adelaide, South Australia, in a suit with no identification. A scrap of paper in a fob pocket read Tamám Shud — 'it is ended' — from the Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam, and a book found nearby contained a line of letters that has never been deciphered.

Maps, diagrams and images

The identification

In 2022, researchers using DNA from the exhumed remains and genealogical databases identified him as Carl Webb, an electrical engineer from Melbourne. The identification was announced by the researchers and has been widely accepted, though some details of his life and death remain unexplained.

The archive files it as live debate because the identity is now known and the circumstances are not.

Where it stands

Documented: the body, the book and the code.

Identified: Carl Webb, by DNA, in 2022.

Not established: the cause and circumstances of death.

Not decoded: the letters.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the case where modern genetics closed the question everyone asked and left the interesting ones open.
