Embodied Cognition

The claim that thinking is shaped by having a body — that concepts are grounded in sensorimotor experience. It is well supported in some domains and over-claimed in others.

The claim

Embodied cognition holds that cognitive processes are shaped by the body's form and its interactions with the environment, rather than being computations run on abstract symbols in the brain. Concepts are grounded in sensorimotor experience; gestures and bodily states affect thought.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Support comes from several directions: action-perception coupling, the way gesture supports learning, the effects of bodily posture on emotion and judgement, and the difficulty of grounding abstract concepts without bodily metaphor. The effects are real and often modest.

The over-claims are the strong versions: that the body does the thinking, or that the brain is not the locus of cognition. Those are philosophical positions rather than findings.

Where it stands

Supported: bodily states and actions influence cognition.

Supported: gesture and sensorimotor grounding in learning.

Contested: how far the influence extends.

Over-claimed: strong versions that displace the brain.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a mainstream position that is often stated in its strongest form, and the evidence supports the middle.
