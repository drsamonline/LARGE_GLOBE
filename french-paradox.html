The French Paradox

A country with high saturated fat and low heart disease. The explanation is still argued.

The observation

Epidemiologists noted that France had lower rates of heart disease than expected from its saturated fat intake, and the phrase 'French paradox' was coined in the 1980s. It was used to promote red wine consumption.

Maps, diagrams and images

The explanations

Proposed explanations include under-reporting of heart deaths, different eating patterns, and the wine. The alcohol explanation is not supported as protective, and later work has questioned whether the paradox is as large as described.

Where it stands

Documented: the original observation.

Questioned: the size of the effect.

Not supported: wine as protective.

Contested: the explanation.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is an epidemiological curiosity that became a marketing campaign.
