The Bog Bodies

Hundreds of bodies have been recovered from northern European peat bogs, preserved by the chemistry of the peat. Many were killed deliberately.

The preservation

Peat bogs are cold, acidic and oxygen-poor, and contain sphagnan, a compound that preserves skin and hair while dissolving bone. Bodies placed in them can survive for thousands of years with skin, hair, fingerprints and stomach contents intact.

Maps, diagrams and images

The deaths

Many of the best-known bodies — Tollund Man, Lindow Man, Grauballe Man, the Yde Girl — show signs of violent death: strangulation, throat cutting, blows to the head. Dating places them in the Iron Age and Roman period, and the pattern has been interpreted as human sacrifice, execution, or punishment.

The evidence is forensic and detailed: last meals, stomach contents, wounds and restraints. The interpretation of why they died is argued; that they died violently is not.

What is established

Documented: the bodies, their preservation and their dates.

Documented: the forensic evidence of violent death.

Contested: sacrifice, execution or punishment.

Documented: last meals and stomach contents.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is archaeology with the bodies still present, and because the forensics are as close to a witness statement as prehistory allows.
