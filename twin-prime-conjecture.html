The Twin Prime Conjecture

There are infinitely many pairs of primes two apart. Nobody can prove it, though we now know there are infinitely many pairs at some fixed distance.

The statement

The conjecture holds that there are infinitely many pairs of primes that differ by two: 3 and 5, 11 and 13, 41 and 43. It is one of the oldest open problems in number theory, and it remains unproved.

The progress

In 2013 Yitang Zhang proved that there are infinitely many pairs of primes separated by some bounded gap — initially 70 million, later reduced by the Polymath project to 246. That is a major result and it is not the conjecture: the bound has not been brought down to 2, and the method does not obviously reach it.

The conjecture is also supported by heuristic arguments and by computation, which is the same kind of support Goldbach has and the same kind that does not constitute proof.

Where it stands

Open: the conjecture itself.

Proved: infinitely many bounded gaps, 2013.

Best bound: 246, from the Polymath project.

Not reached: a gap of 2.

The label: open problem.

Why it is in the archive

Because it shows what mathematical progress looks like when it is real: not a proof, but a bound that moves.
