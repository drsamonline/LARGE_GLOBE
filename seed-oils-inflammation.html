Seed Oils and Inflammation

The claim that industrial seed oils cause inflammation through omega-6 fatty acids. The biochemistry is real; the clinical evidence does not support the harm.

The claim

The claim is that seed oils — sunflower, soybean, canola — are high in linoleic acid, an omega-6 fatty acid that is converted into inflammatory mediators, and that their introduction into the food supply has driven increases in chronic inflammatory disease.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

Linoleic acid is converted to arachidonic acid and to inflammatory eicosanoids in principle. In practice, controlled trials increasing dietary linoleic acid have not shown increased markers of inflammation, and some show reductions. Higher tissue linoleic acid has been associated with lower cardiovascular risk in cohort studies.

The epidemiological correlation with chronic disease is confounded by everything else that changed in the same period, and the trials are the stronger evidence.

Where it stands

Documented: the biochemical pathway.

Not supported: increased inflammation in trials.

Not supported: the disease claim.

Confounded: the ecological correlation.

The honest label: live debate, leaning against the claim.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a claim built from a real pathway to a false conclusion, and because the trials are the part that never gets quoted.
