The Princes in the Tower

Two boys disappeared in the Tower of London in 1483, and their uncle took the throne. Bones found in 1674 were never conclusively identified.

The events

Edward V, aged twelve, and his brother Richard were placed in the Tower of London in 1483. Their uncle, Richard III, had them declared illegitimate and took the crown. The boys were seen less frequently and then not at all; their fate was unknown even at the time.

Maps, diagrams and images

The evidence

In 1674 workmen found a wooden chest containing two skeletons under a staircase in the Tower. Charles II had them placed in Westminster Abbey as the princes. The bones have been examined twice, in 1933 and in 1965, without DNA analysis; permission for modern testing has been refused.

Richard III is the traditional suspect, and Henry VII had motive too. The recent identification of Richard III's remains has not resolved it.

Where it stands

Documented: the boys, their confinement and their disappearance.

Documented: the skeletons and their placement.

Not established: the identity of the skeletons.

Contested: responsibility.

The honest label: live debate.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the physical evidence exists and cannot be tested, and where the political motive is shared by two dynasties.
