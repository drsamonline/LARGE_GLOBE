The Dead Sea Scrolls

Manuscripts hidden in caves near Qumran, including the oldest surviving copies of biblical texts. They were found by Bedouin shepherds.

The find

From 1947, manuscripts were found in caves near Qumran by the Dead Sea. Around 900 scrolls and fragments were recovered, dated between roughly the third century BC and the first century AD, and including the oldest known copies of books of the Hebrew Bible.

Maps, diagrams and images

The significance

The scrolls document the diversity of Judaism in the period, including sectarian texts, and they pushed the textual history of the biblical books back by a millennium. Their publication was slow and contentious, and the last fragments were published decades later.

The archive files it as documented because the manuscripts, the caves and the scholarship are all on the record.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the discovery that changed what we knew about the texts, not just about the place.
