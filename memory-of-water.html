The Memory of Water

A claim that water retains the properties of substances diluted out of existence. The experiment failed a supervised test.

The claim

In 1988 a team led by Jacques Benveniste published in Nature a report that an antibody solution diluted far beyond the point where any molecules remained still produced a biological effect — interpreted as water retaining a memory of the substance.

Maps, diagrams and images

The test

Nature published the paper with an unusual caveat and sent an investigator to repeat the work under supervision. The effect did not survive blinded conditions, and the claim has not been supported. The episode is documented in the journal's own account.

What is established

Documented: the paper.

Documented: the supervised test.

Not reproduced: under blinding.

Not supported: by later work.

The label: debunked.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is the foundation claim of homeopathy tested properly, once.
