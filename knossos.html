Knossos

The Minoan palace excavated by Arthur Evans, reconstructed in concrete, and argued about ever since.

The site

Knossos on Crete was excavated from 1900 by Arthur Evans, revealing a large complex of courts, storerooms and frescoes which he called a palace and attributed to a 'Minoan' civilisation. The site is the largest Bronze Age complex in the Aegean.

Maps, diagrams and images

The controversy

Evans reconstructed parts of the palace in reinforced concrete, painted frescoes in interpretations that are now questioned, and named the civilisation after a myth. The reconstruction is documented and is itself part of the site's history; later work has revised his chronology and his readings.

The archive files it as documented because the excavation and the reconstruction are on the record.

What is established

Documented: the complex and its contents.

Documented: the excavation and the reconstruction.

Contested: the reconstructions' accuracy.

Revised: the chronology.

The label: documented.

Why it is in the archive

Because it is a case where the excavation itself became an artefact that has to be interpreted.
